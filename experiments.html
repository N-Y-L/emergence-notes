Many steps, a smooth law

I let a walker take independent steps of length one, right with probability ppp and left otherwise. The location after nnn steps is random, but its distribution is exactly calculable. Increase the number of steps and compare the discrete answer with a smooth Gaussian.

Mean displacement 0.000; variance 30.000; total probability 1.00000000. Blue dots show exact probability ÷ 2; the dashed red curve is a Gaussian density.

The mean is n(2p−1)n(2p-1)n(2p−1) and the variance is 4np(1−p)4np(1-p)4np(1−p). The exact probability of jjj right steps is (nj)pj(1−p)n−j\binom nj p^j(1-p)^{n-j}(jn​)pj(1−p)n−j at position x=2j−nx=2j-nx=2j−n. I calculate the probabilities by a stable recurrence. Reachable sites are separated by two, so the dots show probability divided by two, which is comparable with a continuous density.

Scope. This is an independent-step model, not molecular dynamics. The Gaussian comparison is an approximation; it can assign nonzero density outside the exact finite range. Step length and time per step are both one.

Oscillators negotiate a shared rhythm

I give 24 oscillators different natural angular frequencies ωi\omega_iωi​, equally spaced from −1 to 1. Each phase θi\theta_iθi​ is an angle marking position within a cycle. Coupling encourages phases to align, while frequency differences pull them apart.

Coherence at time 20: r = 0.947. Seed 17; 24 oscillators. A finite-time result does not establish an infinite-system transition. Dots: oscillator phases; red line: their average vector.

Diffusion can sharpen a difference

I use two interacting concentrations, uuu and vvv, whose spatially uniform steady state is stable without diffusion. If one spreads sufficiently faster than the other, some spatial disturbances can nevertheless grow. I inspect their growth rates before attempting to draw a finished pattern.

Uniform growth rate −0.100. Largest sampled growth 0.057 at k ≈ 0.566. A band of spatial disturbances grows.

In dimensionless units, ∂tu=0.1−u+u2v+∇2u\partial_tu=0.1-u+u^2v+\nabla^2u∂t​u=0.1−u+u2v+∇2u and ∂tv=0.9−u2v+D∇2v\partial_tv=0.9-u^2v+D\nabla^2v∂t​v=0.9−u2v+D∇2v. The uniform state is (u,v)=(1,0.9)(u,v)=(1,0.9)(u,v)=(1,0.9). A small Fourier disturbance of wave number kkk is governed by the matrix (0.8−k21−1.8−1−Dk2)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0.8-k^2&1\\-1.8&-1-Dk^2\end{smallmatrix}\right)(0.8−k2−1.8​1−1−Dk2​). The plotted value is the largest real part of its eigenvalues. A positive value predicts exponential growth in the linear regime.

Scope. This is the linear stability calculation for specified Schnakenberg reaction kinetics. It does not compute the nonlinear saturated stripes or spots. The wave-number scan is continuous and sampled; a finite domain admits only particular modes.

A connected path appears

I independently occupy sites on a 32 by 32 square grid with probability ppp. Occupied sites connect only across a shared edge. A cluster is a set joined by such paths. Local occupancy can then produce a global event: one cluster reaches from the top edge to the bottom.

Top-to-bottom path: absent. Largest cluster contains 197 of 607 occupied sites. One 32 × 32 sample at seed 17; this is not an estimate of the critical occupation probability.

The calculation enumerates every connected cluster exactly for the displayed sample. Boundaries are open, with no wraparound. Reusing a seed keeps the underlying random numbers fixed, so increasing ppp only adds occupied sites. Red squares with white dots mark every spanning cluster.

Scope. A single finite sample has its own first spanning event. Its displayed occupation is not an estimate of an infinite-lattice threshold or a material conductivity. All random draws use the recorded Mulberry32 seed.

A modest local rule, a different global map

I place two arbitrary groups on a 24 by 24 grid with about 20% vacancies. A member requests a move if its same-group fraction among occupied neighbors is below the threshold. This is a precisely specified possibility model: it does not estimate anyone’s preferences or the causes of segregation in a real city.

Same-group fraction among occupied neighbor pairs: 0.502 → 0.803. Unhappy agents remaining: 0. 265 moves in 9 sweeps. Seed 17. No agent currently requests a move.

The eight surrounding sites form the neighborhood, with periodic boundaries. An isolated member is satisfied. Each sweep visits positions in seeded random order; an unhappy member moves to a uniformly chosen vacancy, whether or not that destination will satisfy it. An arrival is not moved again within the same sweep. Blue solid squares and red squares with a white dot distinguish the groups; blank squares are vacancies.

Scope. Population counts are conserved. Runs stop when all members are satisfied or the sweep limit is reached. There are no prices, discriminatory institutions, geography, or group-specific rules. This model cannot apportion real-world causes or justify a policy conclusion.
