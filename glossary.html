A small shared vocabulary

We define specialized terms within the entries. This page collects recurring terms whose meanings are especially easy to slide between fields.

Adaptation
A change that improves performance in a specified environment. In evolution, an adaptation is a trait shaped by selection for its effects; not every useful trait originated for its present use.
Attractor
A set of states approached by nearby trajectories under specified dynamics. Its basin is the set of initial states that approach it.
Bifurcation
A qualitative change in the solutions or their stability as a model parameter varies.
Coarse-graining
A specified removal of detail, such as averaging over small spatial cells, to retain variables useful at a larger scale.
Collective mode
A coordinated pattern of motion or fluctuation involving many constituents.
Conservation law
A statement that a specified quantity remains constant in an isolated system. Within a subregion, that quantity can change by flowing across its boundary.
Correlation
Statistical dependence between fluctuations or events. Correlation alone does not establish the direction or existence of a causal influence.
Critical point
A point in parameter space at which a continuous phase transition occurs, often with a diverging correlation length in the infinite-system limit.
Effective theory
A description designed for a specified range of scales and observables, with the effects of unresolved details encoded in its parameters and rules.
Equilibrium
In thermodynamics, a state with no net macroscopic changes or sustained dissipative currents under the stated constraints. A steady driven state need not be an equilibrium.
Feedback
A process in which a response changes a condition that influences further response. Positive feedback reinforces a perturbation; negative feedback opposes it, locally and under specified conditions.
Fitness
A measure of reproductive contribution in a specified evolutionary model or environment. It is not a general measure of strength, complexity, or moral worth.
Fluctuation
A deviation from a reference value, often an average; its size, timescale, and correlations can contain physical information.
Gene
A DNA sequence whose expression contributes a functional product, often a protein or RNA. What it does depends on cellular context and regulation.
Heritability
A statistical measure of the fraction of variation in a trait attributed to genetic differences in a specified population and environment. It describes variation among individuals, not the genetic share of an individual's trait.
Hysteresis
Dependence of a system’s observed state on its history when control parameters have the same current values.
Instability
Growth of sufficiently small disturbances to a reference state under a specified dynamics.
Macrostate
A description using selected collective variables. Its meaning depends on the chosen level of description.
Microstate
A specification of the detailed variables retained in a model; not necessarily a description at the most fundamental possible level.
Network
A set of nodes and links representing specified relationships. Whether links have direction, weight, or temporal variation must be stated.
Nonlinearity
Failure of a system’s response to a sum of inputs to equal the sum of its separate responses; it permits interactions between disturbances.
Order parameter
A quantity chosen to characterize a kind of ordering or a transition. A useful quantity in one system need not diagnose another.
Phase transition
A qualitative change of state as parameters vary. Equilibrium nonanalyticity is defined in a thermodynamic limit; finite systems show rounded signatures.
Quasiparticle
A sufficiently persistent excitation whose energy, motion, and interactions admit an effective particle description.
Resilience
Ability to remain in or return to a specified regime after disturbance. Local recovery rate and resistance to a change of regime are different measures.
Selection
Differential persistence or reproduction of variants under specified conditions. Natural selection additionally involves inherited variation and reproductive differences.
Self-organization
Formation of organized patterns through interactions within a system under stated surroundings and driving, without specifying the final detailed pattern externally.
Steady state
A state in which selected macroscopic statistics or variables do not change with time; energy or matter may still flow through it.
Symmetry
A transformation that leaves specified laws or observables unchanged. A state can have less symmetry than the laws.
Thermodynamic limit
A limit in which system size and particle number grow while appropriate densities remain fixed.
Topology
Study of properties preserved under a stated class of continuous deformations. Its physical use requires specifying the objects and permitted deformations.
Universality
Agreement of specified large-scale behavior among different microscopic models within a stated class and limit.
