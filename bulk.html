From particles to bulk behavior

A glass of water has temperature, pressure, viscosity, and surface tension. These properties describe distributions and responses of molecules, rather than attributes of an isolated molecule. Their usefulness depends on averaging over enough microscopic motion while retaining the quantities that control the experiment.

Temperature and entropy describe energy exchange and the statistical weight of macroscopic states. Pressure, diffusion, sound, and viscosity follow the transport of conserved quantities. Elasticity and surface tension describe the cost of deformation, while screening shows how the surrounding material modifies a perturbing field. Hydrodynamics combines these ideas: it retains slowly varying fields, constrains their evolution by conservation, and represents faster processes through an equation of state and transport coefficients. This change of description can be effective far from any phase transition.

01Temperature and thermal equilibrium

A gas thermometer and a metal thermometer have different microscopic degrees of freedom, yet they agree after equilibrating with the same bath. Their agreement follows from the quantity that energy exchange equalizes. It need not imply equal energies per particle: rotations, vibrations, interactions, and quantum level spacings all affect how a body stores energy.

Consider two weakly coupled bodies with fixed combined energy EEE. Let ΩA(EA)\Omega_A(E_A)ΩA​(EA​) count states in a narrow energy interval compatible with the other constraints on AAA, and write SA=kBln⁡ΩAS_A=k_B\ln\Omega_ASA​=kB​lnΩA​, where kBk_BkB​ is Boltzmann's constant. Neglecting the interaction energy, the combined state count is proportional to ΩA(EA)ΩB(E−EA)\Omega_A(E_A)\Omega_B(E-E_A)ΩA​(EA​)ΩB​(E−EA​). At a stable maximum,

∂SA∂EA=∂SB∂EB,1T=(∂S∂E)V,N.\frac{\partial S_A}{\partial E_A}=\frac{\partial S_B}{\partial E_B},\qquad \frac1T=\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial E}\right)_{V,N}.∂EA​∂SA​​=∂EB​∂SB​​,T1​=(∂E∂S​)V,N​.

Volume VVV and particle number NNN are held fixed in the derivative. Reciprocal temperature is the entropy slope. For macroscopic bodies with ordinary stable thermodynamics, the state count is sharply concentrated near this maximum, making temperature reproducible despite microscopic fluctuations.

For a small system in a much larger bath, expanding the bath entropy gives SB(E−Ei)≃SB(E)−Ei/TS_B(E-E_i)\simeq S_B(E)-E_i/TSB​(E−Ei​)≃SB​(E)−Ei​/T. Its number of compatible bath states is therefore proportional to e−Ei/(kBT)e^{-E_i/(k_BT)}e−Ei​/(kB​T), the Boltzmann weight of system state iii. The same temperature governs a gas's velocity distribution and a spin's level populations because both exchange energy with the bath. A thermometer measures a property calibrated against this parameter.

This reasoning requires equilibration of the relevant degrees of freedom. In a driven material, electrons and lattice vibrations may exchange energy slowly enough to have different effective temperatures. A single reading then characterizes only the degrees of freedom to which the instrument responds.

02Pressure as momentum transfer

A piston receives separate molecular impacts but feels a nearly steady force. In the local rest frame of an equilibrium fluid, pressure is the normal momentum transferred per unit area and time, averaged over scales large compared with individual collisions. A moving fluid also carries momentum through a surface by bulk advection; this contribution must be separated from pressure.

Take NNN noninteracting particles of mass mmm in a rectangular box of volume VVV. A particle with normal velocity vxv_xvx​ transfers 2m∣vx∣2m|v_x|2m∣vx​∣ when it reflects from a wall. It returns after 2Lx/∣vx∣2L_x/|v_x|2Lx​/∣vx​∣, where LxL_xLx​ is the box length. Summing the average force and dividing by wall area gives

p=NmV⟨vx2⟩=Nm3V⟨v2⟩.p=\frac{Nm}{V}\langle v_x^2\rangle=\frac{Nm}{3V}\langle v^2\rangle.p=VNm​⟨vx2​⟩=3VNm​⟨v2⟩.

The second equality assumes an isotropic velocity distribution. Classical thermal equilibrium gives m⟨v2⟩=3kBTm\langle v^2\rangle=3k_BTm⟨v2⟩=3kB​T, hence pV=NkBTpV=Nk_BTpV=NkB​T. Number density and temperature determine the average momentum flux, although the sequence of impacts remains irregular.

The same accounting applies across an imaginary plane inside the gas: particles crossing it exchange momentum between neighboring regions. Interparticle forces also transmit momentum across such a plane. They contribute substantially in a dense liquid, where the ideal-gas formula no longer applies. In equilibrium, the mechanical pressure agrees with p=−(∂F/∂V)T,Np=-(\partial F/\partial V)_{T,N}p=−(∂F/∂V)T,N​, where the Helmholtz free energy F=E−TSF=E-TSF=E−TS gives the reversible work of changing volume at fixed temperature.

A scalar pressure describes isotropic normal stress. A sheared fluid or a solid generally requires a stress tensor, whose component σij\sigma_{ij}σij​ is the force in direction iii per area across a surface normal to direction jjj. Small sensors also resolve fluctuations hidden by macroscopic averaging. Thus the familiar pressure of a gas is a particularly simple form of the more general transport of momentum.

03Entropy and the direction of relaxation

Remove a partition confining a gas to half a box. The gas spreads, although reversing all particle momenta would reverse the microscopic trajectory. The statistical explanation concerns how much phase space corresponds to each macroscopic description and how the gas was prepared.

A macrostate specifies quantities such as particle counts in spatial cells. A classical microstate specifies all positions and momenta. Boltzmann entropy is SB=kBln⁡ΩS_B=k_B\ln\OmegaSB​=kB​lnΩ, where Ω\OmegaΩ is the phase-space volume compatible with a macrostate, measured in fixed microscopic units. For an ideal gas expanding freely into twice its initial volume, the available positional volume gains a factor 2N2^N2N, so

ΔSB=NkBln⁡2.\Delta S_B=Nk_B\ln 2.ΔSB​=NkB​ln2.

At equilibrium, independently distributed particle positions put all NNN particles in the original half with probability 2−N2^{-N}2−N. This explains why that macrostate is rarely observed; the relaxation time still depends on the dynamics.

The Boltzmann entropy of a macrostate must be distinguished from the fine-grained Gibbs entropy of a distribution. For probability density ρ\rhoρ on phase space with volume element dΓd\GammadΓ, Hamiltonian evolution preserves −kB∫ρln⁡ρ dΓ-k_B\int\rho\ln\rho\,d\Gamma−kB​∫ρlnρdΓ, with a fixed reference measure understood. The distribution develops fine structure and correlations that a macroscopic density measurement does not resolve. For an isolated quantum system, unitary evolution similarly preserves the full-state von Neumann entropy, while a subsystem's entropy can grow through entanglement.

Ordinary relaxation starts from an unusually small region of phase space and evolves toward macrostates occupying much more of it. This argument needs dynamics that actually explores the relevant regions: conserved quantities and restricted motion can prevent equilibration. Reversing an expansion would require preparing the detailed position–momentum correlations generated during its history. Matching the total energy and density alone would not reproduce that exceptional preparation.

04Diffusion from random motion

A dye spot spreads because more molecules cross from a concentrated region into a dilute one than cross back. Individual molecules need no directional bias. A simple model makes the resulting law explicit: a walker takes independent steps +a+a+a or −a-a−a every time interval τ\tauτ, each with probability 1/21/21/2.

The position distribution obeys a discrete update that averages its two neighboring values. Expanding for variation over distances much larger than aaa and times much longer than τ\tauτ gives

∂tP=D∂x2P,D=a22τ,⟨x2⟩=2Dt.\partial_tP=D\partial_x^2P,\qquad D=\frac{a^2}{2\tau},\qquad \langle x^2\rangle=2Dt.∂t​P=D∂x2​P,D=2τa2​,⟨x2⟩=2Dt.

Here the walker starts at the origin, P(x,t)P(x,t)P(x,t) is its probability density, and DDD has units of area per time. A localized initial distribution approaches a Gaussian whose width grows as t\sqrt{t}t​. Transport across twice the distance therefore takes roughly four times as long.

For many particles, conservation gives ∂tc+∇⋅j=0\partial_tc+\nabla\cdot\mathbf j=0∂t​c+∇⋅j=0, where ccc is number concentration and j\mathbf jj is particle flux. Fick's constitutive law, j=−D∇c\mathbf j=-D\nabla cj=−D∇c, closes this equation and reproduces diffusion for constant DDD. In an interacting mixture, the more general driving quantity is chemical potential, the free-energy change per added particle. Its gradient need not point along the concentration gradient.

Ordinary diffusion requires microscopic memory to decay sufficiently quickly. Before velocity correlations decay, motion can be ballistic, with mean squared displacement proportional to t2t^2t2. Persistent correlations or broad waiting-time distributions can instead produce anomalous diffusion, for which that displacement is not proportional to time. Flow and reactions add further transport and source terms. An observed slow expansion should therefore be tested over a range of times before assigning it a small constant diffusivity.

05Sound as a collective density wave

A spoken word crosses a room without carrying the speaker's air along with it. The propagating disturbance consists of local compression and velocity changes. A pressure excess accelerates adjacent fluid, whose motion creates the next compression.

Write density and pressure as ρ0+δρ\rho_0+\delta\rhoρ0​+δρ and p0+δpp_0+\delta pp0​+δp about uniform equilibrium, and let v\mathbf vv be the small mean velocity. To first order, mass and momentum conservation give

∂tδρ+ρ0∇⋅v=0,ρ0∂tv=−∇δp.\partial_t\delta\rho+\rho_0\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=0,\qquad \rho_0\partial_t\mathbf v=-\nabla\delta p.∂t​δρ+ρ0​∇⋅v=0,ρ0​∂t​v=−∇δp.

When heat transfer between neighboring compressed and expanded regions is negligible within a cycle, the response is approximately adiabatic. The equation of state then gives δp=cs2δρ\delta p=c_s^2\delta\rhoδp=cs2​δρ, with cs2=(∂p/∂ρ)sc_s^2=(\partial p/\partial\rho)_scs2​=(∂p/∂ρ)s​ and sss the entropy per unit mass. Combining the equations yields ∂t2δρ=cs2∇2δρ\partial_t^2\delta\rho=c_s^2\nabla^2\delta\rho∂t2​δρ=cs2​∇2δρ.

For an ideal gas, cs2=γp0/ρ0c_s^2=\gamma p_0/\rho_0cs2​=γp0​/ρ0​, where γ=Cp/CV\gamma=C_p/C_Vγ=Cp​/CV​ is the heat-capacity ratio. More generally, sound speed compares the stiffness against compression with the inertia of the fluid. A dense material can transmit sound rapidly if it is correspondingly difficult to compress. The wave's wavelength describes the coordinated density pattern, not the distance traveled by an individual molecule.

Local equilibrium must be established faster than the oscillation, and a wavelength must be large compared with microscopic transport lengths. Viscosity and heat conduction then give small attenuation corrections. Large amplitudes change the local propagation speed and can steepen a wave into a shock. These corrections connect the ideal wave equation to the same momentum and energy transport processes that underlie diffusion and viscosity.

06Viscosity and the diffusion of momentum

Moving one plate past another with fluid between them requires a force even when the plates never touch. Faster fluid layers transfer momentum to slower neighbors. For a Newtonian fluid, the resulting viscous stress is linear in the local rate of deformation.

In simple shear,

σxy=η ∂yvx.\sigma_{xy}=\eta\,\partial_yv_x.σxy​=η∂y​vx​.

Here vxv_xvx​ is velocity along the plates, yyy measures distance across the gap, and σxy\sigma_{xy}σxy​ is tangential stress across a surface normal to yyy. The dynamic shear viscosity η\etaη has units of pascal seconds. Steady flow between plates of area AAA, separation hhh, and relative speed UUU, with no slip at either plate and no imposed pressure gradient, has a linear velocity profile and requires force F=ηAU/hF=\eta AU/hF=ηAU/h.

In a dilute gas, molecules transport momentum between collisions, giving the estimate η∼ρvthℓ\eta\sim\rho v_{\rm th}\ellη∼ρvth​ℓ. Here ρ\rhoρ is mass density, vthv_{\rm th}vth​ a thermal speed, and ℓ\ellℓ the mean free path. Increasing density shortens ℓ\ellℓ, approximately compensating for the larger number of momentum carriers. In a dense liquid, intermolecular forces and structural rearrangements dominate the relaxation instead. Different microscopic mechanisms can therefore produce the same leading stress law.

In this shear flow, the work becomes heat at rate η(∂yvx)2\eta(\partial_yv_x)^2η(∂y​vx​)2 per volume. Momentum disturbances spread with kinematic viscosity ν=η/ρ\nu=\eta/\rhoν=η/ρ, which has the same dimensions as a diffusion coefficient. A polymer solution may retain stress after the motion stops because stretched molecules relax slowly. Such memory requires a time-dependent constitutive relation; a constant viscosity is adequate only when those internal relaxations are fast compared with the experiment.

07Elasticity and persistent constraints

A metal rod returns nearly to its initial length after a small pull because deforming its collective atomic arrangement raises the free energy. Its atoms continue to vibrate throughout. Elasticity describes displacements relative to a stable reference configuration, on time scales over which irreversible rearrangements are negligible.

Let u(r)\mathbf u(\mathbf r)u(r) be the displacement of material initially at r\mathbf rr. The small-strain tensor ϵij=(∂iuj+∂jui)/2\epsilon_{ij}=(\partial_i u_j+\partial_j u_i)/2ϵij​=(∂i​uj​+∂j​ui​)/2 measures changes of lengths and angles while removing infinitesimal rigid rotations. Expanding about an unstressed configuration gives

F−F0=12∫Cijklϵijϵkl dV,σij=Cijklϵkl.F-F_0=\frac12\int C_{ijkl}\epsilon_{ij}\epsilon_{kl}\,dV,\qquad \sigma_{ij}=C_{ijkl}\epsilon_{kl}.F−F0​=21​∫Cijkl​ϵij​ϵkl​dV,σij​=Cijkl​ϵkl​.

Repeated indices are summed. The elastic stiffness tensor CijklC_{ijkl}Cijkl​ relates strain to stress; σij\sigma_{ij}σij​ is force in direction iii per area across a surface normal to jjj. A linear stress law follows from a quadratic expansion of free energy, even when the underlying atomic forces are nonlinear.

For an isotropic solid, two moduli suffice: the bulk modulus penalizes volume change and the shear modulus penalizes distortion at fixed volume. Crystal symmetry generally permits additional independent coefficients. These coefficients include thermal effects as well as bond energetics. In rubber, stretching a polymer network reduces the number of accessible chain configurations, so much of the restoring force is entropic.

An ordinary liquid continually rearranges under sustained shear; a solid retains a restoring stress on the measurement time scale. A material may therefore respond elastically during a rapid pull but flow under a slow load. Glasses achieve finite-time rigidity without periodic atomic order, while defects and bond rearrangements allow permanent deformation at larger loads. The elastic field remains useful as long as its reference configuration persists through the experiment.

08Surface tension and curved interfaces

A small free drop tends to become spherical because creating liquid–vapor interface costs free energy. Surface molecules have different surroundings from molecules in the interior. The resulting surface tension γ\gammaγ is the reversible free-energy cost per added area under specified thermodynamic conditions.

For a clean interface with approximately constant γ\gammaγ, its free-energy contribution is Fs=γAF_s=\gamma AFs​=γA. A spherical drop of radius RRR has dA=8πR dRdA=8\pi R\,dRdA=8πRdR and dV=4πR2 dRdV=4\pi R^2\,dRdV=4πR2dR. Balancing surface work against the pressure difference gives

Δp dV=γ dA,Δp=2γR.\Delta p\,dV=\gamma\,dA,\qquad \Delta p=\frac{2\gamma}{R}.ΔpdV=γdA,Δp=R2γ​.

This Young–Laplace relation says that the interior pressure is higher, with a greater jump for a smaller drop. It applies to one spherical interface; a thin soap bubble has two. At a general smooth interface, the pressure jump is γ\gammaγ times the sum of its two principal curvatures, with signs fixed by the chosen normal.

At fixed volume, a sphere minimizes surface area. Gravity becomes competitive over the capillary length ℓc=γ/(Δρg)\ell_c=\sqrt{\gamma/(\Delta\rho g)}ℓc​=γ/(Δρg)​, where Δρ\Delta\rhoΔρ is the density difference between phases and ggg gravitational acceleration. Small drops are therefore rounded, while larger ones can be strongly deformed by gravity. On a solid substrate, the free energies of the solid–liquid and solid–vapor interfaces also determine the equilibrium contact angle.

Surface tension includes both energetic and entropic effects. Molecules that accumulate at an interface can alter it; spatial gradients of γ\gammaγ exert tangential stresses and drive flow. The treatment of an interface as a sharp surface with a constant coefficient becomes inaccurate when the drop radius approaches the molecular interfacial thickness.

09Screening by mobile charges

A charge in an electrolyte attracts opposite ions and repels ions of the same sign. Their redistribution weakens its field at large distances. The measured potential therefore belongs to the inserted charge together with the response of the medium.

Consider ions of charges +q+q+q and −q-q−q, each with bulk number density n0n_0n0​, in a solvent of permittivity ε\varepsilonε. At temperature TTT, dilute-ion Boltzmann distributions give n±=n0exp⁡(∓qϕ/kBT)n_\pm=n_0\exp(\mp q\phi/k_BT)n±​=n0​exp(∓qϕ/kB​T) in potential ϕ\phiϕ. For ∣qϕ∣≪kBT|q\phi|\ll k_BT∣qϕ∣≪kB​T, the induced charge density is q(n+−n−)≃−2n0q2ϕ/(kBT)q(n_+-n_-)\simeq-2n_0q^2\phi/(k_BT)q(n+​−n−​)≃−2n0​q2ϕ/(kB​T). Outside the inserted charge, Poisson's equation becomes

∇2ϕ=ϕλD2,λD=εkBT2n0q2.\nabla^2\phi=\frac{\phi}{\lambda_D^2},\qquad \lambda_D=\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon k_BT}{2n_0q^2}}.∇2ϕ=λD2​ϕ​,λD​=2n0​q2εkB​T​​.

The Debye length λD\lambda_DλD​ measures the screening distance. In three dimensions, linear response to a point charge QQQ gives ϕ(r)=Qe−r/λD/(4πεr)\phi(r)=Qe^{-r/\lambda_D}/(4\pi\varepsilon r)ϕ(r)=Qe−r/λD​/(4πεr). At fixed permittivity and density, higher temperature weakens the ionic concentration around the source and lengthens the screening distance. More mobile ions shorten it.

The calculation is self-consistent: the potential changes the ion distribution, and the redistributed ions contribute to that same potential. Using the bare charge's potential throughout would miss this feedback. In the infinite-medium solution, the integrated induced charge compensates QQQ; finite systems must also satisfy their boundary and total-charge constraints.

For a point source, linearization necessarily fails sufficiently close to the origin. The exponential expression describes the weak-field region; a nonlinear core can change its amplitude. Finite ion sizes and strong ionic correlations can further invalidate the dilute mean-field treatment. Metals screen through quantum electronic occupations and may have oscillatory responses, so screening in general need not have the Debye form.

10Hydrodynamics from slow conserved fields

Density, velocity, and temperature can predict large-scale fluid motion without specifying molecular coordinates. Conservation explains why these fields remain important: a conserved quantity leaves a region through its boundary, so a fluctuation spread over a long distance generally relaxes slowly.

For a one-component fluid, mass, momentum, and energy provide the basic fields. Local equilibrium assumes that microscopic degrees of freedom relax within a region much smaller than the scale of macroscopic variation. An equilibrium equation of state then relates local pressure, density, and temperature. With mass density ρ\rhoρ and local mean velocity v\mathbf vv, mass conservation is ∂tρ+∇⋅(ρv)=0\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf v)=0∂t​ρ+∇⋅(ρv)=0.

For an incompressible Newtonian fluid with constant mass density ρ\rhoρ and shear viscosity η\etaη, momentum balance becomes

ρ[∂tv+(v⋅∇)v]=−∇p+η∇2v+f,∇⋅v=0.\rho\big[\partial_t\mathbf v+(\mathbf v\cdot\nabla)\mathbf v\big]=-\nabla p+\eta\nabla^2\mathbf v+\mathbf f,\qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf v=0.ρ[∂t​v+(v⋅∇)v]=−∇p+η∇2v+f,∇⋅v=0.

Here f\mathbf ff is force per volume. The advective term (v⋅∇)v(\mathbf v\cdot\nabla)\mathbf v(v⋅∇)v accounts for acceleration as fluid moves into a region of different velocity. The viscous term follows from the constitutive relation between stress and velocity gradients. Conservation fixes the balance equation; it does not fix the numerical viscosity. In the incompressible problem, pressure also enforces the constraint ∇⋅v=0\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=0∇⋅v=0.

The same equations describe chemically different liquids once their coefficients and boundary conditions are supplied. Molecular confinement or driving faster than local relaxation can remove the required separation of scales. Slowly relaxing composition or orientation must be added as extra fields in mixtures or liquid crystals. Even with few fields, nonlinear advection can generate complex vortices and turbulence. The reduction is in the information needed to specify the dynamics, not necessarily in the difficulty of solving it.
