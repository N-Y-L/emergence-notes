Quantum matter makes new excitations

The effective particles in matter need not coincide with its microscopic constituents. A phonon is a quantum of coordinated atomic motion. A Fermi-liquid quasiparticle carries electronic charge but has a dispersion and lifetime modified by the surrounding electrons. A fractional quantum Hall quasihole has charge and exchange statistics that emerge from the correlated many-electron state.

The chapter follows these excitations and the states that support them. Bose condensation describes macroscopic occupation of an orbital, while superfluidity and superconductivity concern phase stiffness and response. Mott insulation and Anderson localization suppress transport through interactions and coherent disorder scattering, respectively. Intrinsic topological order and topological bands then show two different ways in which quantum states carry structure beyond an ordinary local order parameter. In each case, identifying the excitation or response is the first step toward determining the low-energy description and its range of validity.

21Fermi liquids and long-lived quasiparticles

Many metals behave at low temperature like electron gases with altered masses and interactions, even when microscopic repulsion is substantial. Landau's Fermi-liquid theory describes their low-energy excitations as quasiparticles: particles dressed by the response of the surrounding electrons, with a sufficiently narrow energy width to remain identifiable.

A Fermi surface separates occupied and unoccupied momentum states at zero temperature in a noninteracting gas. In a Fermi liquid, a corresponding surface survives. For volume VVV, quasiparticle energy ϵp∗\epsilon^*_{\mathbf p}ϵp∗​, and residual interaction fpp′f_{\mathbf p\mathbf p'}fpp′​, the energy change due to small occupation changes δnp\delta n_{\mathbf p}δnp​ can be expanded as

δE=∑pϵp∗δnp+12V∑p,p′fpp′δnpδnp′.\delta E=\sum_{\mathbf p}\epsilon^*_{\mathbf p}\delta n_{\mathbf p}+\frac{1}{2V}\sum_{\mathbf p,\mathbf p'}f_{\mathbf p\mathbf p'}\delta n_{\mathbf p}\delta n_{\mathbf p'}.δE=p∑​ϵp∗​δnp​+2V1​p,p′∑​fpp′​δnp​δnp′​.

Spin labels are suppressed. A quasiparticle retains the electron's charge and, in a spin-rotation-invariant setting, its spin quantum number. Its velocity differs from the bare value. The interaction term also modifies compressibility and magnetic susceptibility, so replacing the electron mass alone is insufficient.

Pauli exclusion blocks most final scattering states near the Fermi surface. In a conventional three-dimensional Fermi liquid, the quasiparticle energy width behaves as Γ∝ϵ2+(πkBT)2\Gamma\propto\epsilon^2+(\pi k_BT)^2Γ∝ϵ2+(πkB​T)2, where ϵ\epsilonϵ is measured from the chemical potential. At zero temperature, Γ/∣ϵ∣→0\Gamma/|\epsilon|\to0Γ/∣ϵ∣→0 as ϵ→0\epsilon\to0ϵ→0: lower-energy excitations become better defined. The same description gives a heat capacity linear in temperature, with coefficient set by the quasiparticle density of states.

This low-energy organization can fail in one dimension, at some quantum critical points, or when another phase intervenes. Spectral line widths and thermodynamic responses must therefore agree with the quasiparticle picture. A fitted effective mass by itself does not establish that the corresponding excitation is long lived.

22Phonons: quantized lattice vibrations

A neutron can scatter from a crystal and transfer a definite energy without adding or removing an atom. It can instead create a phonon, one quantum of a collective vibrational mode.

For a periodic chain of atoms with mass MMM, spacing aaa, displacement uju_juj​, momentum pjp_jpj​, and nearest-neighbor spring constant κ\kappaκ, the harmonic Hamiltonian and mode frequency ωk\omega_kωk​ at wave number kkk are

The normal modes labeled by kkk are coordinated patterns that oscillate independently. Transforming to these coordinates makes HHH a sum of harmonic oscillators, with energies ℏωk(nk+1/2)\hbar\omega_k(n_k+1/2)ℏωk​(nk​+1/2). Increasing integer nkn_knk​ by one adds a phonon.

A mode extends through the crystal, but superposing nearby wave numbers produces a wave packet that propagates at group velocity dωk/dkd\omega_k/dkdωk​/dk. No atom travels with it across the sample. A one-phonon state has zero mean displacement; its energy appears in displacement and momentum fluctuations. A classical lattice wave is instead represented by a coherent superposition of occupation numbers with an oscillating mean displacement. Quantizing a collective coordinate therefore introduces distinct quantum states of the same atomic motion.

At small ∣k∣a|k|a∣k∣a, ωk≃aκ/M∣k∣\omega_k\simeq a\sqrt{\kappa/M}|k|ωk​≃aκ/M​∣k∣, recovering continuum sound. Anharmonicity allows phonons to interact and decay. A particle description remains useful when a mode's width is small relative to its frequency. Crystal momentum ℏk\hbar kℏk is defined modulo a reciprocal-lattice momentum and is not generally the sample's total mechanical momentum. Long-lived modes provide a compact account of energy transport and scattering without requiring the motion of every atom to be tracked.

23Bose–Einstein condensation and macroscopic occupation

Bose–Einstein condensation is macroscopic occupation of a one-particle orbital. More precisely, trace out all but one particle and normalize the resulting one-particle density matrix to trace NNN, the total particle number. Its matrix elements describe single-particle coherence between positions, and its eigenvalues are orbital occupations that sum to NNN. Condensation occurs when one eigenvalue is proportional to NNN. For a uniform ideal gas this state is the zero-momentum orbital. The macroscopic occupation describes a many-particle state; it does not bind the particles into one object.

For noninteracting bosons of mass mmm, the occupation of a level of energy ϵ\epsilonϵ is [e(ϵ−μ)/(kBT)−1]−1[e^{(\epsilon-\mu)/(k_BT)}-1]^{-1}[e(ϵ−μ)/(kB​T)−1]−1. Taking the lowest energy as zero, the chemical potential μ\muμ approaches zero from below as the gas is cooled. In three dimensions, the excited states can then hold at most

nex=ζ(3/2)λT3,λT=2πℏ2mkBT.n_{\rm ex}=\frac{\zeta(3/2)}{\lambda_T^3},\qquad \lambda_T=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\hbar^2}{mk_BT}}.nex​=λT3​ζ(3/2)​,λT​=mkB​T2πℏ2​​.

Here nexn_{\rm ex}nex​ is excited-state number density, λT\lambda_TλT​ the thermal wavelength, and ζ(3/2)=∑j=1∞j−3/2\zeta(3/2)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{-3/2}ζ(3/2)=∑j=1∞​j−3/2. At total density nnn, the transition satisfies nλT3=ζ(3/2)n\lambda_T^3=\zeta(3/2)nλT3​=ζ(3/2). Below this temperature TcT_cTc​, particles exceeding the thermal capacity occupy the ground state. Writing the ground-state population as N0N_0N0​, the condensate fraction is N0/N=1−(T/Tc)3/2N_0/N=1-(T/T_c)^{3/2}N0​/N=1−(T/Tc​)3/2.

This saturation mechanism requires Bose statistics but no attraction. Collisions are nevertheless important for equilibrating experimental gases. Penrose and Onsager's density-matrix criterion extends the definition to interacting or nonuniform systems: an eigenvalue of order NNN identifies condensation, and its eigenfunction identifies the occupied orbital. That orbital need not be a plane wave.

The first dilute rubidium experiments observed a narrow component whose population grew on cooling. Trap geometry changes the available excited states, so the uniform-gas formulas do not apply unchanged. A uniform ideal gas in one or two dimensions has no finite-temperature condensation in the thermodynamic limit. Condensation also differs from superfluidity: occupation is a property of the state, whereas persistent flow depends on its response and accessible excitations.

24Superfluidity: excitations and persistent flow

A fluid current decays by transferring energy and momentum to other degrees of freedom. Superfluid flow can persist because the excitation spectrum and the possible changes of its collective phase restrict those decay processes.

For a homogeneous fluid moving past a weak obstacle, an excitation of momentum p\mathbf pp and rest-frame energy ϵ(p)\epsilon(\mathbf p)ϵ(p) has energy balance ϵ(p)−v⋅p\epsilon(\mathbf p)-\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf pϵ(p)−v⋅p. Emission cannot lower the energy if this remains positive. For an isotropic spectrum, Landau's criterion is

v<vL,vL=min⁡p>0ϵ(p)p.v<v_L,\qquad v_L=\min_{p>0}\frac{\epsilon(p)}p.v<vL​,vL​=p>0min​pϵ(p)​.

This tests a specified excitation channel, rather than proving stability against every possible disturbance.

For a dilute Bose gas of particles of mass mmm, with repulsive contact coupling ggg and number density nnn, Bogoliubov theory gives ϵ(p)=[p2/(2m)][p2/(2m)+2gn]\epsilon(p)=\sqrt{[p^2/(2m)][p^2/(2m)+2gn]}ϵ(p)=[p2/(2m)][p2/(2m)+2gn]​. At low momentum it becomes cpcpcp, with c=gn/mc=\sqrt{gn/m}c=gn/m​, and this spectrum gives vL=cv_L=cvL​=c. The ideal gas has quadratic excitations and zero Landau threshold. Condensation alone has therefore not supplied this energetic obstruction to decay.

Phase stiffness is the free-energy cost of a spatial variation in the collective phase θ\thetaθ. For a neutral condensate, vs=(ℏ/m)∇θ\mathbf v_s=(\hbar/m)\nabla\thetavs​=(ℏ/m)∇θ. Single-valuedness quantizes circulation around a loop avoiding vortex cores in units of h/mh/mh/m. Changing the winding requires the phase to become undefined somewhere along the process, for example when a vortex crosses the flowing channel. Such a phase slip connects states that cannot be joined by a small smooth phase deformation.

Boundaries, obstacles, and thermal fluctuations can enable vortex nucleation at velocities below the elementary-excitation estimate. Different channels can thus have different critical velocities for the same fluid. The superfluid fraction measured by flow or rotational response also need not equal the condensate fraction: phase rigidity, occupation, and metastability are related but distinct properties.

25Superconductivity and electromagnetic phase stiffness

A superconductor exhibits the Meissner effect: in an appropriate equilibrium regime, magnetic field is expelled from its bulk. This response distinguishes it from a hypothetical perfect conductor, which would preserve previously trapped flux when its resistance vanished.

In conventional superconductors, lattice vibrations can mediate an effective attraction between electrons near the Fermi surface. The Bardeen–Cooper–Schrieffer state coherently combines many overlapping Cooper pairs. For isotropic pairing, let ξk\xi_{\mathbf k}ξk​ be normal-state energy relative to the chemical potential and Δ\DeltaΔ the gap. The fermionic excitation energies are

Ek=ξk2+∣Δ∣2,E_{\mathbf k}=\sqrt{\xi_{\mathbf k}^2+|\Delta|^2},Ek​=ξk2​+∣Δ∣2​,

Pairing changes the excitation spectrum, while phase stiffness determines the long-wavelength current response.

Write an effective pair field as Ψ=npeiθ\Psi=\sqrt{n_p}e^{i\theta}Ψ=np​​eiθ. With pair charge qp=−2eq_p=-2eqp​=−2e, its gauge-invariant momentum is ℏ∇θ−qpA\hbar\nabla\theta-q_p\mathbf Aℏ∇θ−qp​A. For effective pair mass mpm_pmp​, a local continuum model with a positive quadratic cost for this momentum gives

j=npqpmp(ℏ∇θ−qpA).\mathbf j=\frac{n_pq_p}{m_p}(\hbar\nabla\theta-q_p\mathbf A).j=mp​np​qp​​(ℏ∇θ−qp​A).

The ratio np/mpn_p/m_pnp​/mp​ parametrizes superfluid stiffness; in a material it need not be obtained by simply counting microscopic pairs. Taking a curl in a vortex-free region gives ∇×j=−(npqp2/mp)B\nabla\times\mathbf j=-(n_pq_p^2/m_p)\mathbf B∇×j=−(np​qp2​/mp​)B. Maxwell's equations then imply exponential field penetration with length λ=mp/(μ0npqp2)\lambda=\sqrt{m_p/(\mu_0n_pq_p^2)}λ=mp​/(μ0​np​qp2​)​ in SI units, where μ0\mu_0μ0​ is the vacuum permeability. The phase depends on gauge, but this magnetic response does not.

Type-II superconductors can admit quantized flux vortices, whose motion dissipates energy. Strong current, temperature, or field can also destroy the superconducting response. Unconventional superconductors may have gap nodes and pairing mechanisms different from lattice-mediated attraction. The common macroscopic feature is electromagnetic phase stiffness; the isotropic BCS construction supplies one microscopic realization.

26Mott insulation and the separation of charge and spin scales

A partially filled noninteracting band normally permits conduction. Strong interactions can instead make charge transfer costly and produce a Mott insulator. The one-orbital Hubbard model isolates this competition:

H=−t∑⟨ij⟩,σ(ciσ†cjσ+h.c.)+U∑ini↑ni↓.H=-t\sum_{\langle ij\rangle,\sigma}(c^\dagger_{i\sigma}c_{j\sigma}+\mathrm{h.c.})+U\sum_i n_{i\uparrow}n_{i\downarrow}.H=−t⟨ij⟩,σ∑​(ciσ†​cjσ​+h.c.)+Ui∑​ni↑​ni↓​.

Here ciσ†c^\dagger_{i\sigma}ciσ†​ creates an electron of spin σ\sigmaσ at site iii, ciσc_{i\sigma}ciσ​ removes one, and niσ=ciσ†ciσn_{i\sigma}=c^\dagger_{i\sigma}c_{i\sigma}niσ​=ciσ†​ciσ​ is the number operator. The parameters ttt and U>0U>0U>0 are nearest-neighbor hopping energy and the cost of double occupancy. The Hermitian conjugate supplies reverse hopping. At one electron per site, hopping tends to create a doubly occupied site and an empty site. It gains kinetic energy but costs interaction energy.

In the atomic limit t=0t=0t=0, the cost is explicit. When UUU is large compared with the bandwidth, the range of noninteracting band energies, finite hopping need not restore mobile charge. A useful diagnostic is the charge gap

Δc=E0(N+1)+E0(N−1)−2E0(N),\Delta_c=E_0(N+1)+E_0(N-1)-2E_0(N),Δc​=E0​(N+1)+E0​(N−1)−2E0​(N),

where E0(N)E_0(N)E0​(N) is the ground-state energy with NNN electrons. A positive gap in the thermodynamic limit separates particle-addition and particle-removal thresholds. Meanwhile, virtual hopping generates spin exchange of order 4t2/U4t^2/U4t2/U. Low-energy spin excitations can remain active far below the charge-transfer scale.

The effective low-energy variables are then local moments rather than freely moving electrons. Magnetic order may accompany this state, but the strong-coupling charge gap and an ordering-induced band gap have different origins. Filling matters as much as interaction strength: the same argument does not guarantee insulation away from integer occupancy. Additional orbitals and lattice motion complicate real materials. Ultracold bosons in optical lattices offer a separate realization in which repulsion suppresses number fluctuations at integer filling, with different particle statistics but the same basic competition between hopping and interaction energy.

27Anderson localization by coherent scattering

Random scattering can slow a classical random walk, but a coherent wave can cease spreading altogether. Interference among repeated scattering paths produces Anderson localization even without classical barriers enclosing each localized state.

For a one-dimensional lattice, the stationary amplitude ψj\psi_jψj​ obeys

Eψj=−t(ψj+1+ψj−1)+Vjψj.E\psi_j=-t(\psi_{j+1}+\psi_{j-1})+V_j\psi_j.Eψj​=−t(ψj+1​+ψj−1​)+Vj​ψj​.

The hopping energy is ttt and the static on-site energies VjV_jVj​ are independent random variables. Without disorder, eigenstates are extended waves. For generic uncorrelated disorder in this model, eigenstates are exponentially localized, with envelope ∣ψj∣∼e−∣j−j0∣a/ξ|\psi_j|\sim e^{-|j-j_0|a/\xi}∣ψj​∣∼e−∣j−j0​∣a/ξ. Here j0j_0j0​ is a localization center, aaa the lattice spacing, and ξ\xiξ the localization length. Weak disorder can make ξ\xiξ much larger than an experimental sample.

A wave packet is a superposition of eigenstates. When the participating states are localized, their evolving phases do not produce ordinary indefinite diffusion. Replacing every scattering amplitude by an independent probability would discard the interference responsible for this result. In three dimensions, localized and extended states can occur at different energies, separated by a mobility edge. The dimensional and symmetry assumptions therefore belong to the localization prediction.

Saturated wave-packet expansion and exponentially suppressed transmission are useful signatures, provided absorption and finite-time trapping are distinguished from coherent localization. Absorption removes wave intensity; localization redistributes a conserved wave into spatially confined states. Inelastic scattering, time-dependent disorder, or interactions can restore transport by opening additional processes. Localization of an interacting many-body system is a separate question. The single-particle result already shows how a wave interacting with many scattering sites can acquire a collective transport length without requiring many occupied particles.

28Fractional charge and anyonic statistics

A two-dimensional electron fluid can support a localized excitation of charge +e/3+e/3+e/3, although every electron has charge −e-e−e. The excitation is a deficit spread across a correlated many-electron state. Fractionalization assigns new quantum numbers to that collective excitation without subdividing an electron.

Laughlin's state at filling ν=1/m\nu=1/mν=1/m, with odd integer m≥3m\geq3m≥3, makes the construction explicit. Filling is the ratio of electron number to the degeneracy of a Landau level, a quantized cyclotron-energy level. With zj=xj+iyjz_j=x_j+iy_jzj​=xj​+iyj​ and magnetic length ℓB=ℏ/(e∣B∣)\ell_B=\sqrt{\hbar/(e|B|)}ℓB​=ℏ/(e∣B∣)​, a suitable field convention gives

Ψm∝∏i<j(zi−zj)mexp⁡ ⁣[−∑i∣zi∣24ℓB2].\Psi_m\propto\prod_{i<j}(z_i-z_j)^m\exp\!\left[-\sum_i\frac{|z_i|^2}{4\ell_B^2}\right].Ψm​∝i<j∏​(zi​−zj​)mexp[−i∑​4ℓB2​∣zi​∣2​].

The zeros suppress close electron pairs and reduce repulsive interaction energy. Multiplying by ∏i(zi−η)\prod_i(z_i-\eta)∏i​(zi​−η) creates a quasihole near η\etaη. In the screened Laughlin-fluid regime, its integrated electron deficit carries charge +e/m+e/m+e/m. The electron number is unchanged; compensating charge lies elsewhere, for example at the droplet edge.

Arovas, Schrieffer, and Wilczek evaluated the geometric phase of slowly moving quasiholes. After subtracting ordinary electromagnetic and dynamical phases, taking one quasihole fully around another contributes statistical phase of magnitude 2π/m2\pi/m2π/m. An exchange has magnitude π/m\pi/mπ/m, with sign set by orientation and field convention. These are Abelian anyons: exchanges act by commuting scalar phases, rather than the bosonic or fermionic exchange signs of the constituents.

The calculation requires a gapped correlated bulk and well-separated quasiholes moved slowly enough to avoid other excitations. The wavefunction is exact for special interactions and approximates a phase realized more generally. Interferometry at ν=1/3\nu=1/3ν=1/3 has observed phase slips consistent with the predicted full-winding phase, in devices designed to suppress charging effects. Charge, spatial profile, and interference thus provide distinct tests of the emergent excitation.

29Intrinsic topological order in the toric code

Different quantum ground states can agree on every local measurement while differing in observables that wind around the whole system. Kitaev's toric code gives an exactly solvable example of intrinsic topological order, with both nonlocal state structure and fractionalized excitations.

Place a spin-1/21/21/2 on every edge of a square lattice with periodic boundaries in both directions. This gives a torus, with Hamiltonian

H=−Je∑sAs−Jm∑pBp.H=-J_e\sum_s A_s-J_m\sum_p B_p.H=−Je​s∑​As​−Jm​p∑​Bp​.

The operator AsA_sAs​ is the product of Pauli XXX operators on edges meeting vertex sss; BpB_pBp​ is the product of Pauli ZZZ operators around plaquette ppp, an elementary square. They commute because a vertex and plaquette share either zero or two edges. Positive Je,JmJ_e,J_mJe​,Jm​ favor As=Bp=+1A_s=B_p=+1As​=Bp​=+1.

These local constraints leave four ground states on the torus. Operators in a small contractible region cannot distinguish them, while loops winding around the two independent torus directions can. A string of Pauli ZZZ operations creates vertex violations at its endpoints; a dual-lattice string of Pauli XXX operations creates plaquette violations. Separating either pair costs no additional energy. Taking an electric-type endpoint around a magnetic-type endpoint multiplies the state by −1-1−1. These labels refer to two kinds of constraint violation, not physical electric and magnetic charges.

Weak local perturbations that preserve the bulk gap generally split the ground states by an amount exponentially small in system size. Changing a nonlocal state label requires an operation extending around the system, which accounts for this suppression. Thermal errors are different: a pair of excitations can be created and then separate without a growing energy barrier. For weak local coupling to a thermal bath, the two-dimensional toric code does not become a stable passive quantum memory merely by increasing its size at fixed nonzero temperature.

This intrinsic order differs from the topology of an ordinary band insulator. Its ground-state degeneracy and mutual anyonic statistics belong to the correlated many-body state and cannot be captured by a conventional local order parameter.

30Topological bands and protected boundaries

An insulating bulk can constrain its boundary to carry electronic states. To distinguish this from accidental surface conduction, we need a bulk property that cannot change continuously while the relevant gap and symmetries remain intact.

For a two-dimensional noninteracting band insulator, the occupied Bloch states define a geometry over crystal momentum. Berry curvature Ωn(k)\Omega_n(\mathbf k)Ωn​(k) measures the geometric phase of band nnn around an infinitesimal momentum-space loop. Its integral over the Brillouin zone gives the total Chern number:

C=12π∑n occupied∫BZΩn(k) d2k,σxy=Ce2h.C=\frac1{2\pi}\sum_{n\,\mathrm{occupied}}\int_{\rm BZ}\Omega_n(\mathbf k)\,d^2k,\qquad \sigma_{xy}=C\frac{e^2}{h}.C=2π1​noccupied∑​∫BZ​Ωn​(k)d2k,σxy​=Che2​.

The integer CCC cannot change under a smooth deformation that keeps occupied and empty bands separated. Here σxy\sigma_{xy}σxy​ is zero-temperature Hall conductivity, eee is the elementary charge, and hhh is Planck's constant; the sign follows the chosen orientation. Haldane's model demonstrates that a nonzero CCC is possible with zero net magnetic flux per unit cell.

Across an interface, the difference in Chern numbers fixes the net number of boundary branches moving in one direction minus those moving oppositely. This is bulk–boundary correspondence. An edge with an unpaired directed branch is chiral: static disorder has no reverse-moving partner into which that branch can scatter at the same edge. The statement constrains boundary propagation, while contacts and other channels still affect measured resistance.

Protection can also rely on symmetry. A quantum spin Hall insulator has counterpropagating edge partners related by time reversal. For a single pair, time-reversal-preserving single-particle disorder cannot elastically backscatter one partner into the other. Magnetic perturbations, coupling opposite edges, and suitable interaction processes can change that conclusion. Mercury-telluride quantum wells provide experimental evidence for this boundary conduction. Unlike intrinsic topological order, this band structure requires neither fractionalized charge nor topological ground-state degeneracy on a torus.

  • Kane and Mele (2005), Z2 Topological Order and the Quantum Spin Hall Effect. Time-reversal-invariant band topology and quantum spin Hall edge states.
  • König et al. (2007), Quantum Spin Hall Insulator State in HgTe Quantum Wells. Transport evidence for quantum spin Hall boundaries in HgTe wells.
