Collective order and its limits

Interactions can select an arrangement that persists over distances far larger than the particle spacing. Crystals organize positions, ferromagnets align magnetic moments, and antiferromagnets alternate them. An order parameter is an observable chosen to distinguish these arrangements: the average that detects one kind of order can miss another completely.

This chapter develops that choice of observable and the physics it reveals. Liquid crystals retain directional order while flowing; phase separation organizes composition. Near a continuous transition, growing correlations lead to critical scaling, whose common features can be explained by the renormalization group. The last examples extend the picture: broken continuous symmetries produce soft modes, vortex binding distinguishes the two sides of a BKT transition, and glasses develop rigidity when structural relaxation becomes too slow to observe. These forms of organization have different thermodynamic and dynamical origins.

11Crystallization: free energy and nucleation

Cooling a liquid can produce a repeating density pattern with a definite orientation. The atomic positions are selected collectively: translating a free crystal as a whole leaves its energy unchanged, while displacing one atom relative to its neighbors generally costs energy.

For NNN particles at positions rj\mathbf r_jrj​, the static structure factor at scattering wavevector q\mathbf qq detects positional correlations:

S(q)=1N⟨∣∑j=1Neiq⋅rj∣2⟩.S(\mathbf q)=\frac1N\left\langle\left|\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r_j}\right|^2\right\rangle.S(q)=N1​⟨​j=1∑N​eiq⋅rj​​2⟩.

At reciprocal-lattice vectors, which match the crystal periodicity, amplitudes from many particles add coherently. The resulting Bragg peaks distinguish long-range periodic order from the short-range correlations of an ordinary liquid.

At fixed temperature and pressure, equilibrium favors the phase with lower Gibbs free energy G=E+pV−TSG=E+ pV-TSG=E+pV−TS. Interaction energy and entropy both matter. Alder and Wainwright's hard-sphere simulations provided a particularly clear example: particles that only exclude overlap can crystallize without any attraction. At high density, an ordered arrangement can allow enough additional particle motion to outweigh the entropy lost by selecting lattice positions. A regular-looking snapshot can therefore belong to the phase with greater total entropy at fixed volume and energy.

Thermodynamic preference does not guarantee immediate crystallization. In a simple nucleation approximation, a spherical crystal region in a metastable liquid has

ΔG(R)=4πR2γ−4π3R3Δg,\Delta G(R)=4\pi R^2\gamma-\frac{4\pi}{3}R^3\Delta g,ΔG(R)=4πR2γ−34π​R3Δg,

where γ>0\gamma>0γ>0 is interfacial tension and Δg>0\Delta g>0Δg>0 the bulk free-energy advantage per volume. The barrier peaks at R=2γ/ΔgR=2\gamma/\Delta gR=2γ/Δg: smaller nuclei tend to shrink, while larger ones can grow. Impurities, interfacial anisotropy, and cooling rate alter this route. They help determine whether the sample crystallizes and which defects remain, even when its equilibrium phase is already known.

12Ferromagnetism and spontaneous symmetry breaking

A ferromagnetic domain retains a collective orientation without a continuing applied field. The Ising model isolates one mechanism by assigning a spin si=±1s_i=\pm1si​=±1 to each of NNN lattice sites, representing a moment constrained to one axis:

H=−J∑⟨ij⟩sisj−h∑isi,m=1N∑i⟨si⟩.H=-J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}s_is_j-h\sum_i s_i,\qquad m=\frac1N\sum_i\langle s_i\rangle.H=−J⟨ij⟩∑​si​sj​−hi∑​si​,m=N1​i∑​⟨si​⟩.

The first sum runs over neighbors, J>0J>0J>0 favors parallel spins, and hhh is field energy per spin. At h=0h=0h=0, reversing all spins leaves HHH unchanged. An ordered state with a selected sign of magnetization mmm breaks this symmetry: its orientation is not shared by every state with the same energy.

Flipping a region inside an ordered state creates a domain wall of unfavorable neighbor pairs. Ordering depends on the competition between the wall's energy and the number of ways walls can occur. Replacing each neighbor by its mean gives m=tanh⁡[(zJm+h)/(kBT)]m=\tanh[(zJm+h)/(k_BT)]m=tanh[(zJm+h)/(kB​T)], where zzz is the coordination number. This mean-field approximation predicts a transition but misses spatial fluctuations. The nearest-neighbor chain has no ordered phase at nonzero temperature, whereas Onsager's exact square-lattice solution establishes a finite-temperature transition. Dimensionality changes the balance between excitation cost and entropy.

For a finite zero-field system, an equilibrium average over both orientations gives m=0m=0m=0. Spontaneous magnetization is defined by taking the thermodynamic limit before removing an infinitesimal selecting field. Long-range spin correlations also reveal the order.

Real ferromagnets add exchange anisotropy and long-range magnetic fields. Forming oppositely magnetized domains can reduce the external field energy, leaving little net sample moment. Distinguishing such a sample from a disordered magnet therefore requires spatial correlations or domain-sensitive measurements, not only the total magnetization.

13Antiferromagnetism and staggered order

Regularly alternating magnetic moments can have zero net magnetization. On a bipartite lattice, divided into sublattices AAA and BBB with every nearest-neighbor bond joining unlike sublattices, the alternating pattern is detected by comparing their moments.

For spin operators Si\mathbf S_iSi​, the antiferromagnetic Heisenberg model is H=J∑⟨ij⟩Si⋅SjH=J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\mathbf S_i\cdot\mathbf S_jH=J∑⟨ij⟩​Si​⋅Sj​, with J>0J>0J>0. For NNN sites divided equally between the sublattices, define

M=1N∑i⟨Si⟩,L=1N∑iηi⟨Si⟩,\mathbf M=\frac1N\sum_i\langle\mathbf S_i\rangle,\qquad \mathbf L=\frac1N\sum_i\eta_i\langle\mathbf S_i\rangle,M=N1​i∑​⟨Si​⟩,L=N1​i∑​ηi​⟨Si​⟩,

where ηi=+1\eta_i=+1ηi​=+1 on AAA and −1-1−1 on BBB. The uniform magnetization M\mathbf MM may vanish while the staggered magnetization L\mathbf LL remains nonzero. This is Néel order. Magnetic diffraction detects it at wavevectors matching the alternation, even when a magnetometer records almost no total moment.

One microscopic origin of the exchange is the half-filled, one-orbital Hubbard model. Electrons hop with amplitude ttt and pay energy UUU for double occupancy. For U≫tU\gg tU≫t, real charge transfer is costly, but virtual hopping through doubly occupied intermediate states lowers the energy of antiparallel configurations. Second-order perturbation theory gives J≃4t2/UJ\simeq4t^2/UJ≃4t2/U. Charge motion can therefore be suppressed while spins still interact on a much smaller energy scale. Magnons are quantized collective oscillations of the ordered spins.

The lattice and symmetry determine whether Néel order survives. A triangle cannot make all three neighboring classical moments antiparallel; this incompatibility is frustration. Strong quantum fluctuations can also prevent order. At nonzero temperature, an isotropic two-dimensional model with short-range exchange has no conventional magnetic long-range order; anisotropy or interlayer coupling can allow real layered materials to order. The relevant observable must follow the spatial organization favored by these interactions.

14Nematic liquid crystals: alignment without a lattice

A nematic liquid crystal flows while retaining a preferred molecular axis. Its molecules continually change neighbors, but their orientations remain correlated. This distinguishes orientational order from the repeating positional order of a crystal.

For rods whose two ends are equivalent, n\mathbf nn and −n-\mathbf n−n describe the same axis. A vector average can therefore vanish in an aligned sample. In three dimensions, the appropriate order parameter is the tensor

Qij=⟨uiuj−δij3⟩,Q_{ij}=\left\langle u_i u_j-\frac{\delta_{ij}}3\right\rangle,Qij​=⟨ui​uj​−3δij​​⟩,

where u\mathbf uu is a molecular unit axis. An isotropic distribution has Q=0Q=0Q=0. For a uniaxial nematic, Qij=S(ninj−δij/3)Q_{ij}=S(n_i n_j-\delta_{ij}/3)Qij​=S(ni​nj​−δij​/3), where n\mathbf nn is the director, an unoriented axis, and SSS measures alignment. This form respects the head–tail symmetry.

Alignment need not be caused by attractive forces. In Onsager's model of long rigid hard rods, nearly perpendicular rods exclude more volume from one another than nearly parallel rods. At sufficient concentration, losing orientational entropy can release enough positional freedom to lower the total free energy. The model makes an entropic origin of directional order explicit, much as hard spheres demonstrate an entropic route to crystallization.

Spatial variations of the director cost elastic free energy. Splay makes neighboring axes diverge, twist rotates the director between neighboring planes, and bend curves its direction along itself. Boundaries and electric fields can select the director and thereby control the optical response. Defects occur where the surrounding orientation pattern cannot be continued smoothly through a core without reducing the order. The tensor and these deformation costs provide a continuum description of an orientationally ordered fluid; polar or layered liquid-crystal phases require additional variables.

15Phase separation and spinodal instability

A binary mixture can prefer two different compositions to one uniform composition. Whether separation starts from small fluctuations or requires a finite nucleus depends on the local stability of the initial mixture.

Let c(r)c(\mathbf r)c(r) be the local fraction of one component. A minimal free energy is

With constant mobility M>0M>0M>0 and flux j=−M∇μ\mathbf j=-M\nabla\muj=−M∇μ, composition conservation gives the Cahn–Hilliard equation ∂tc=M∇2μ\partial_tc=M\nabla^2\mu∂t​c=M∇2μ. A Fourier perturbation about uniform c0c_0c0​ has growth rate

Flow, elastic stress, reactions, or long-range interactions can modify either process and can arrest coarsening.

16Criticality and growing correlations

Near a liquid–gas critical point, density fluctuations can scatter visible light and make the fluid cloudy. Their growth reflects the weakening of the restoring force against long-wavelength density changes. At a conventional continuous transition, the correlation length diverges in the ideal infinite equilibrium system.

For a scalar order parameter ϕ\phiϕ, the connected correlation function at separation rrr is G(r)=⟨ϕ(r)ϕ(0)⟩−⟨ϕ⟩2G(r)=\langle\phi(\mathbf r)\phi(0)\rangle-\langle\phi\rangle^2G(r)=⟨ϕ(r)ϕ(0)⟩−⟨ϕ⟩2. Subtracting the mean isolates fluctuations. Away from criticality, GGG typically decays exponentially beyond a correlation length ξ\xiξ. Near a short-range critical point,

ξ∼∣t∣−ν,G(r)∼r−(d−2+η)(a≪r≪ξ).\xi\sim|t|^{-\nu},\qquad G(r)\sim r^{-(d-2+\eta)}\quad(a\ll r\ll\xi).ξ∼∣t∣−ν,G(r)∼r−(d−2+η)(a≪r≪ξ).

Here t=(T−Tc)/Tct=(T-T_c)/T_ct=(T−Tc​)/Tc​, ddd is dimension, aaa a microscopic length, and ν,η\nu,\etaν,η critical exponents. A widening interval between aaa and ξ\xiξ admits approximately scale-free correlations.

A Gaussian free energy illustrates the softening:

F=12∫[r0ϕ2+κ∣∇ϕ∣2]dV.F=\frac12\int[r_0\phi^2+\kappa|\nabla\phi|^2]dV.F=21​∫[r0​ϕ2+κ∣∇ϕ∣2]dV.

For r0,κ>0r_0,\kappa>0r0​,κ>0, a Fourier mode has variance proportional to kBT/(r0+κk2)k_BT/(r_0+\kappa k^2)kB​T/(r0​+κk2). As r0r_0r0​ decreases, long waves become inexpensive and ξ=κ/r0\xi=\sqrt{\kappa/r_0}ξ=κ/r0​​ grows. Interactions between fluctuations, omitted here, change the critical exponents in many physical systems.

Response grows with the correlated region: a small conjugate field can change many degrees of freedom together. Relaxation also commonly slows as those regions reorganize. Finite size and finite observation time cut off this growth and round thermodynamic singularities. Establishing a critical description therefore requires consistent scaling of correlations and response as a control parameter approaches the transition; a fitted power law in one observable supplies only part of that evidence.

17Universality and the renormalization group

A fluid near its liquid–gas endpoint and a uniaxial magnet can share critical exponents despite different constituents and forces. Universality concerns specified long-distance observables after material-dependent units and amplitudes have been accounted for.

The renormalization group averages over short-distance fluctuations and rescales lengths. Effective coupling constants change under this operation. A fixed point is an effective description reproduced by the transformation. Wilson's explanation of universality is that different microscopic models can approach the same fixed point and therefore acquire the same critical scaling.

Near a fixed point, a small coupling perturbation ggg transforms to leading order as

g′≃byg,g'\simeq b^y g,g′≃byg,

with length rescaling factor b>1b>1b>1. A relevant perturbation has y>0y>0y>0 and grows; an irrelevant one has y<0y<0y<0 and diminishes. For y=0y=0y=0, the perturbation is marginal, and nonlinear terms in its evolution must be examined. Temperature distance from criticality is usually relevant, explaining why the transition must be approached by tuning. Many details of short-range interactions are irrelevant, explaining why their differences do not change leading exponents.

Spatial dimension, order-parameter symmetry, interaction range, disorder, and additional soft fields help determine the accessible fixed points. A scalar liquid–gas endpoint can have static Ising critical behavior after the appropriate thermodynamic variables are identified. Its dynamics need not match a magnet's: a conserved density relaxes differently from a nonconserved magnetization.

Experiments also have a finite range of lengths and temperatures. A weak perturbation that is relevant may grow appreciably only at very large scales, producing crossover between apparent scaling regimes. Predicting universality therefore requires both identifying a fixed point and checking which physically allowed perturbations destabilize it.

18Goldstone modes and the cost of a slow twist

An ordered phase may select an orientation even though its Hamiltonian favors none. Uniformly rotating that state then costs no energy. Neighboring regions rotated by different amounts do cost energy, but their mismatch becomes arbitrarily small when the variation is spread over a long wavelength. Goldstone modes are the resulting gapless collective excitations of a broken continuous global symmetry.

For an angular coordinate θ(r)\theta(\mathbf r)θ(r), invariance under uniform shifts excludes a local restoring term proportional to θ2\theta^2θ2. With stiffness KKK, the leading static cost is instead

F[θ]=K2∫∣∇θ∣2dV.F[\theta]=\frac K2\int|\nabla\theta|^2dV.F[θ]=2K​∫∣∇θ∣2dV.

A sinusoidal distortion has energy density proportional to Kk2Kk^2Kk2 times its squared amplitude, where kkk is wave number. Introducing an inertial coefficient χ\chiχ, the Lagrangian density L=χ(∂tθ)2/2−K∣∇θ∣2/2\mathcal L=\chi(\partial_t\theta)^2/2-K|\nabla\theta|^2/2L=χ(∂t​θ)2/2−K∣∇θ∣2/2 gives frequency ω=K/χ∣k∣\omega=\sqrt{K/\chi}|k|ω=K/χ​∣k∣. Its quantum excitation energy vanishes as k→0k\to0k→0.

Symmetry constrains the soft coordinate, but the dynamics determines the dispersion. An isotropic ferromagnet has a quadratic long-wavelength magnon dispersion rather than this linear one. In nonrelativistic systems, two broken generators can act as conjugate variables of a single mode; Watanabe and Murayama's counting result makes this distinction precise. Broken crystal rotations likewise need not add independent modes beyond those already described by displacement fields.

A weak anisotropy or selecting field adds a cost for uniform rotation and can open a gap. Long-range forces and dynamical gauge fields modify the elementary argument as well. The existence of the ordered state must also be checked: thermal fluctuations can destroy continuous-symmetry order in low dimensions. Within these conditions, the long-wavelength spectrum follows from the symmetries and dynamics of the collective coordinate.

19Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition: unbinding vortices

Planar spins in an infinite two-dimensional system with short-range isotropic interactions cannot sustain ordinary magnetization at nonzero temperature. Long-wavelength angular fluctuations destroy it. A sharp transition can nevertheless distinguish bound vortex pairs from free vortices.

A vortex has integer winding qqq: the spin angle changes by 2πq2\pi q2πq around a loop enclosing its core. Smooth deformations preserve that integer until a core crosses the loop or the angular field becomes undefined. With phase stiffness ρs\rho_sρs​, the long-distance free energy is F=(ρs/2)∫∣∇θ∣2d2rF=(\rho_s/2)\int|\nabla\theta|^2d^2rF=(ρs​/2)∫∣∇θ∣2d2r. A unit vortex has ∣∇θ∣=1/r|\nabla\theta|=1/r∣∇θ∣=1/r outside core radius aaa, hence

Ev≃πρsln⁡(L/a)+Ecore.E_v\simeq\pi\rho_s\ln(L/a)+E_{\rm core}.Ev​≃πρs​ln(L/a)+Ecore​.

The system size is LLL and EcoreE_{\rm core}Ecore​ is the core energy. Approximately (L/a)2(L/a)^2(L/a)2 possible vortex positions provide entropy 2kBln⁡(L/a)2k_B\ln(L/a)2kB​ln(L/a). Energy and positional entropy therefore compete at the same logarithmic order.

Below the Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition, opposite vortices remain paired. Spin correlations decay algebraically because smooth fluctuations persist; this is quasi-long-range order. Above the transition, unbound vortices produce exponential decay. The single-vortex estimate identifies the competition, while the effect of vortex pairs must be included in the effective long-distance stiffness. Just below the transition it obeys

ρs(Tc−)=2kBTcπ,\rho_s(T_c^-)=\frac{2k_BT_c}{\pi},ρs​(Tc−​)=π2kB​Tc​​,

the universal jump predicted by Nelson and Kosterlitz in this stiffness normalization.

The same reasoning applies to appropriate two-dimensional neutral superfluids. Finite size cuts off the logarithms, while interlayer coupling, anisotropy, or electromagnetic screening can change the transition. Measurements of vortex separation, correlations, and stiffness test different parts of the mechanism. The topology here is the winding of a classical field, distinct from intrinsic topological order in a quantum many-body state.

20Glasses and the slowing of structural relaxation

A glass resists shear without the repeating atomic pattern of a crystal. Cooling a liquid while avoiding crystallization can make its structural relaxation time τα\tau_\alphaτα​ exceed the experiment's duration. Rearrangements that would relax a liquid's stress then occur too slowly for the sample to flow appreciably.

The operational glass transition occurs when τα(T)\tau_\alpha(T)τα​(T) becomes comparable with a preparation or observation time. Its measured temperature therefore depends on cooling rate. Once out of equilibrium, the material can age: its properties continue changing with elapsed time after preparation. Neither finite-time arrest nor aging alone identifies an equilibrium thermodynamic singularity.

Relaxation is spatially heterogeneous. A particle can be confined by its neighbors, and escape may require several neighbors to move. To quantify this, let wi(t)=1w_i(t)=1wi​(t)=1 if particle iii moves less than a specified microscopic distance during time ttt, and zero otherwise. For NNN particles, define Q(t)=N−1∑iwi(t)Q(t)=N^{-1}\sum_iw_i(t)Q(t)=N−1∑i​wi​(t) and

χ4(t)=N[⟨Q(t)2⟩−⟨Q(t)⟩2].\chi_4(t)=N\big[\langle Q(t)^2\rangle-\langle Q(t)\rangle^2\big].χ4​(t)=N[⟨Q(t)2⟩−⟨Q(t)⟩2].

Expanding the variance expresses χ4\chi_4χ4​ as a sum of correlations between local mobilities. Independently rearranging particles contribute only local fluctuations; groups moving together add correlated contributions. Its normalization and interpretation depend on the ensemble and displacement threshold, so it is not automatically a unique correlation volume.

Competing theories explain growing barriers through structural organization, cooperative rearrangements, or dynamical facilitation, in which a mobile region enables nearby motion. Kinetically constrained models show explicitly how restricted moves can generate slow heterogeneous dynamics despite simple equilibrium statistics. Distinguishing these explanations requires more than fitting the average relaxation time: spatial mobility correlations and responses to changed preparation provide additional tests. An ideal equilibrium glass transition remains a separate theoretical question from the established observation of kinetic arrest.
