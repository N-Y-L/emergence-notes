Sources and scope

I attach a source to the result it supports. Primary experiments and theoretical papers appear alongside identified reviews, lecture notes, and institutional explanations. A result about a model does not establish that the model explains every similar-looking pattern in nature.

This is a curated atlas, not an exhaustive systematic review. Mathematical results, empirical findings, and open interpretations retain their different status. The framework is motivated in part by Anderson’s “More Is Different” (1972).

From particles to bulk behavior

1. Why two different thermometers agree

Sources and what they establish
  • Tong, Statistical Physics, §1.2.2 and §1.3. Authoritative lecture derivation of temperature from entropy maximization and the canonical distribution; equilibrium assumptions apply.

2. From molecular impacts to a smooth force

Sources and what they establish
  • Feynman, Leighton and Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics, I §39–2. Textbook derivation of dilute-gas pressure as momentum transfer and the ideal-gas equation of state.

3. Why mixing has a direction

Sources and what they establish
  • Tong, Statistical Physics, §1.2.1 and §1.3.3. Lecture definitions of Boltzmann and Gibbs entropy; the distinction between microscopic reversibility and coarse observations is developed explicitly here.

4. How aimless steps make a predictable profile

Sources and what they establish
  • Feynman, Leighton and Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics, I Ch. 43. Textbook treatment of diffusion from microscopic transport and concentration gradients.
  • Feynman, Leighton and Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics, I §41–4. Independent-step random walk and growth of mean squared displacement.

5. A disturbance moves while matter stays nearby

Sources and what they establish
  • Feynman, Leighton and Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics, I Ch. 47. Derivation of the acoustic wave equation and the adiabatic rather than isothermal sound speed.

6. Friction without rubbing solid surfaces

Sources and what they establish
  • Feynman, Leighton and Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics, II §41–1 and §41–2. Definition of shear viscosity and the viscous stress tensor.
  • Feynman, Leighton and Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics, I Ch. 43. Molecular transport estimates, including momentum transport and viscosity.

7. How a solid remembers its shape

Sources and what they establish
  • Feynman, Leighton and Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics, II Ch. 39. Elastic tensor, quadratic strain energy, microscopic stiffness, and limits of Hooke’s law.
  • MIT, Electronic and Mechanical Properties of Materials, Elasticity lectures. Authoritative course notes distinguish bonding-based elasticity in crystals and glasses from entropic elasticity in rubber.

8. Why a drop behaves as if it has a skin

Sources and what they establish
  • MIT, Advanced Fluid Dynamics, Section 11: Surface Tension. Official course notes identify energetic/mechanical definitions, Young–Laplace balance, and gravity and wetting effects.
  • Bush, Interfacial Phenomena, Lecture 3: Fluid Statics. Derives curvature-pressure balance and the capillary length for a meniscus.

9. How many charges hide one charge

Sources and what they establish
  • Hutchinson, Introduction to Plasma Physics I, §1.2.3: Debye Shielding. Official lecture materials cover Debye shielding; the two-mobile-species electrolyte coefficient is derived explicitly in this entry.

10. Why a few fields can replace an enormous state

Sources and what they establish
  • Feynman, Leighton and Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics, II Ch. 40. Derives continuity and material acceleration for continuum flow and states incompressibility assumptions.
  • Feynman, Leighton and Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics, II §41–2. Adds viscous stress to momentum balance; source for the Newtonian constitutive relation.

Collective order and its limits

11. How a liquid chooses a lattice

Sources and what they establish
  • Alder and Wainwright (1957), Phase Transition for a Hard Sphere System. Primary molecular-dynamics report of the hard-sphere transition; supports that attractive forces are not required.
  • Feynman, Leighton and Sands, The Feynman Lectures on Physics, II Ch. 30. Textbook explanation of crystal periodicity, reciprocal geometry, and defects; not used for the entropy-based hard-sphere argument.

12. A common direction without a common command

Sources and what they establish
  • Onsager (1944), Crystal Statistics. I. A Two-Dimensional Model with an Order-Disorder Transition. Primary exact zero-field square-lattice Ising result; establishes a transition beyond mean-field reasoning.
  • Tong, Statistical Field Theory, §1.1–§1.3. Lecture treatment of order parameters, mean-field theory, and the importance of dimensionality.

13. Order that cancels its own average

Sources and what they establish
  • Néel (1970), Nobel Lecture. Primary retrospective explanation of opposite sublattice magnetizations and the Néel ordering temperature.
  • Hubbard (1963), Electron correlations in narrow energy bands. Primary interaction model underlying the stated large-U virtual-hopping argument; the exchange formula is a restricted strong-coupling consequence, not a general material law.

14. Keeping direction while losing position

Sources and what they establish
  • Onsager (1949), The Effects of Shape on the Interaction of Colloidal Particles. Foundational slender-rod model of orientational ordering from excluded volume; the approximation applies to sufficiently long, rigid rods.
  • MIT, Electromagnetic Energy: Liquid Crystal Display Technology, Lecture 26. Official lecture material on liquid-crystal structure, direction-dependent response, and electrically controlled orientation.
  • University of Michigan, Lecture 7: Excluded Volume. Authoritative lecture summary of Onsager’s hard-rod excluded-volume model and its self-consistent orientational distribution.

15. When uniformity becomes unstable

Sources and what they establish
  • Cahn and Hilliard (1958), Free Energy of a Nonuniform System. I. Interfacial Free Energy. Primary gradient-free-energy construction; the dynamical stability and dissipation results are derived in this entry, rather than attributed to the equilibrium paper.

16. Fluctuations that lose a preferred size

Sources and what they establish
  • Tong, Statistical Field Theory, §2.2: Correlation Functions. Lecture derivation of Gaussian correlations, correlation length, and critical opalescence.
  • Tong, Statistical Field Theory, §3.2.1. Lecture scaling relations distinguish interacting critical exponents from the Gaussian approximation.

17. Why different materials can share exponents

Sources and what they establish
  • Wilson (1982), The Renormalization Group and Critical Phenomena, Nobel Lecture. Primary account by a developer of the renormalization-group explanation of universality and critical scaling.
  • Tong, Statistical Field Theory, §3. Authoritative lecture definitions of fixed points and relevant, irrelevant, and marginal couplings.

18. The soft motion left by a chosen direction

Sources and what they establish
  • Watanabe and Murayama (2012), Unified Description of Nambu-Goldstone Bosons without Lorentz Invariance. Primary effective-theory result on nonrelativistic Goldstone counting and different dispersions.
  • Watanabe and Murayama (2013), Redundancies in Nambu-Goldstone Bosons. Primary explanation of why broken crystal rotations do not require independent modes beyond phonons.

19. A transition made by unbinding vortices

Sources and what they establish
  • Kosterlitz and Thouless (1973), Ordering, metastability and phase transitions in two-dimensional systems. Primary transition mechanism; its historical phrase “topological order” is distinguished here from modern intrinsic quantum topological order.
  • Nelson and Kosterlitz (1977), Universal Jump in the Superfluid Density of Two-Dimensional Superfluids. Primary prediction of the universal jump in the renormalized stiffness.

20. Rigidity without a repeating pattern

Sources and what they establish
  • Berthier et al. (2005), Direct experimental evidence of a growing length scale accompanying the glass transition. Primary experimental evidence based on multipoint dynamical susceptibilities; does not prove a unique microscopic mechanism or an ideal equilibrium transition.
  • Garrahan and Chandler (2002), Geometrical explanation and scaling of dynamical heterogeneities in glass forming systems. Primary kinetically constrained model analysis; supports facilitation as an explicit mechanism in models, not as a universal established explanation.

Quantum matter makes new excitations

21. Why interacting electrons can look nearly free

Sources and what they establish
  • Shankar (1994), Renormalization-group approach to interacting fermions. Author’s research account of the Fermi-liquid fixed point and instabilities; dimensional and low-energy restrictions matter.
  • Coleman, Many-Body Physics: The Unfinished Frontier. Authoritative lecture treatment of dressed fermionic quasiparticles and their low-energy description.

22. A particle made from many moving atoms

Sources and what they establish
  • Tong, Solid State Physics, Ch. 4: Phonons. Authoritative normal-mode and quantum-vibration construction; the packet and number-state distinctions are explicitly explained in this entry.

23. When one quantum state becomes macroscopic

Sources and what they establish
  • Tong, Statistical Physics, §3.5: Bose–Einstein Condensation. Lecture derivation of ideal-gas excited-state saturation and dimensional dependence.
  • Anderson et al. (1995), Observation of Bose-Einstein Condensation in a Dilute Atomic Vapor. Primary trapped-rubidium experiment; its evidence is not represented as a test of the uniform-gas condensate-fraction formula.

24. When flow cannot easily shed its energy

Sources and what they establish
  • Dalibard (2016), Interacting gases and the Landau criterion, Collège de France. Authoritative lecture on interactions, metastable currents, Bogoliubov theory, and the Landau condition.
  • Floerchinger, Quantum Field Theory 1, Lecture 14. Authoritative derivation of the Bogoliubov dispersion and its Landau sound-speed threshold.

25. A current-carrying state with magnetic rigidity

Sources and what they establish
  • Bardeen, Cooper and Schrieffer (1957), Theory of Superconductivity. Primary microscopic pairing theory for conventional superconductivity; the isotropic gap formula is used within that scope.
  • MIT, Applied Superconductivity, Lecture 10. Official lecture on the supercurrent equation, London equations, fluxoid quantization, and vortices.

26. An insulator made by the cost of sharing a site

Sources and what they establish
  • Hubbard (1963), Electron correlations in narrow energy bands. Primary model of on-site electronic correlations and the atomic-to-band limits.
  • Greiner et al. (2002), Quantum phase transition from a superfluid to a Mott insulator in a gas of ultracold atoms. Primary bosonic optical-lattice experiment; explicitly distinguished from an electronic Hubbard material.

27. When scattering prevents a wave from spreading

Sources and what they establish
  • Anderson (1958), Absence of Diffusion in Certain Random Lattices. Primary demonstration that randomness can prevent quantum diffusion; the one-dimensional specialization is stated with its standard assumptions.
  • Abrahams et al. (1979), Scaling Theory of Localization: Absence of Quantum Diffusion in Two Dimensions. Primary scaling account of dimensional differences in noninteracting disordered transport; assumptions about symmetry and coherence remain essential.

28. A fraction of charge without a fraction of an electron

Sources and what they establish
  • Laughlin (1983), Anomalous Quantum Hall Effect: An Incompressible Quantum Fluid with Fractionally Charged Excitations. Primary trial-state and fractionally charged quasihole construction.
  • Arovas, Schrieffer and Wilczek (1984), Fractional Statistics and the Quantum Hall Effect. Primary adiabatic Berry-phase calculation of fractional statistics.
  • Nakamura et al. (2020), Direct observation of anyonic braiding statistics. Primary interferometry evidence at filling 1/3; the measured full-winding phase is distinguished from an exchange phase.

29. Distinct states that local measurements cannot tell apart

Sources and what they establish
  • Kitaev (2003; preprint 1997), Fault-tolerant quantum computation by anyons. Primary solvable lattice construction, nonlocal ground-state structure, and anyonic excitations; the entry concerns the model, not a claim of its realization in a natural material.
  • Castelnovo and Chamon (2007), Entanglement and topological entropy of the toric code at finite temperature. Primary finite-temperature analysis clarifying the distinction between finite-size low-temperature behavior and the thermodynamic limit; supports the thermal limitation.

30. Why an insulating interior can require a conducting edge

Sources and what they establish
  • Haldane (1988), Model for a Quantum Hall Effect without Landau Levels. Primary noninteracting lattice model with quantized Hall response and no net flux.
  • Kane and Mele (2005), Z2 Topological Order and the Quantum Spin Hall Effect. Primary time-reversal-invariant band classification and gapless edge prediction; historical title is not identified with intrinsic topological order.
  • König et al. (2007), Quantum Spin Hall Insulator State in HgTe Quantum Wells. Primary transport evidence for edge conduction and sensitivity to time-reversal breaking.

Rhythms, patterns, and failure

31. How different clocks find a common rhythm

Sources and what they establish
  • Strogatz (2000), From Kuramoto to Crawford: exploring the onset of synchronization in populations of coupled oscillators. Review of the Kuramoto model and its synchronization threshold; the threshold refers to specified distributions and coupling assumptions.
  • Kuramoto (1984), Cooperative dynamics of oscillator community: A study based on lattice of rings. Primary treatment of phase reduction for interacting limit-cycle oscillators and collective dynamical order; its phase-coupling assumptions define the model’s scope.

32. A chemical mixture that keeps time

Sources and what they establish
  • Field and Noyes (1974), Oscillations in chemical systems. IV. Limit cycle behavior in a model of a real chemical reaction. Primary Oregonator model: reduced chemical steps produce an attracting closed concentration trajectory; it is a model of a specific reaction mechanism.

33. When diffusion creates a pattern

Sources and what they establish
  • Turing (1952), The chemical basis of morphogenesis. Primary mathematical construction of diffusion-driven instability; its biological applications are hypotheses requiring system-specific evidence.

34. A fluid organizes its own circulation

Sources and what they establish
  • Cross and Hohenberg (1993), Pattern formation outside of equilibrium. Authoritative review of driven pattern formation, including Rayleigh–Bénard convection and near-onset descriptions.
  • Rayleigh (1916), On convection currents in a horizontal layer of fluid, when the higher temperature is on the under side. Primary linear stability calculation with buoyancy, viscosity, and thermal conduction under specified boundary conditions; it does not determine fully developed nonlinear patterns.

35. Unpredictability without random forcing

Sources and what they establish
  • Lorenz (1963), Deterministic nonperiodic flow. Primary convection truncation demonstrating bounded nonperiodic dynamics sensitive to initial conditions; not a complete atmospheric forecast model.

36. A wave that behaves like a persistent object

Sources and what they establish
  • Zabusky and Kruskal (1965), Interaction of “Solitons” in a Collisionless Plasma and the Recurrence of Initial States. Primary numerical study of persistent nonlinear dispersive pulses and their interactions in the Korteweg–de Vries equation.

37. Order in the statistics of irregular motion

Sources and what they establish
  • Kolmogorov (1941), The local structure of turbulence in incompressible viscous fluid for very large Reynolds numbers. Primary similarity hypotheses for small-scale turbulence; applicability requires the stated high-Reynolds-number and local-statistical assumptions.

38. One small change, many rearrangements

Sources and what they establish
  • Sethna, Dahmen, and Myers (2001), Crackling noise. Review of impulsive multiscale responses and scaling, illustrated by disorder-driven magnetic models; it does not assert one mechanism for all crackling systems.
  • Sethna et al. (1993), Hysteresis and hierarchies: Dynamics of disorder-driven first-order phase transformations. Primary zero-temperature random-field Ising model of driven spin avalanches; critical behavior requires appropriate disorder and field, rather than following from slow driving alone.

39. Rigidity without a crystal

Sources and what they establish
  • O’Hern, Silbert, Liu, and Nagel (2003), Jamming at zero temperature and zero applied stress: The epitome of disorder. Primary numerical study of frictionless repulsive particles, preparation-dependent thresholds, mechanical response, and soft modes; no universal grain-packing density is inferred.

40. How a tiny crack controls a large solid

Sources and what they establish
  • Griffith (1921), The phenomena of rupture and flow in solids. Primary energetic theory of brittle fracture and flaw sensitivity; plastic dissipation and dynamic crack selection require extensions.

Living matter: structures that maintain and reproduce themselves

41. How a membrane assembles itself

Sources and what they establish
  • Israelachvili, Mitchell & Ninham (1976), Theory of self-assembly of hydrocarbon amphiphiles into micelles and bilayers. Primary theoretical paper; connects thermodynamics, interaction free energies, and molecular geometry to micelles and bilayers.
  • Alberts et al. (2002), The Chemical Components of a Cell. Textbook account of amphiphilic cell components and noncovalent interactions; biological context, not a claim that cells are equilibrium assemblies.

42. A molecular chain becomes an entropic spring

Sources and what they establish
  • Bustamante et al. (1994), Entropic elasticity of lambda-phage DNA. Primary paper bibliographic record; the freely jointed-chain calculation here is an explicit pedagogical model, not a fit reported in this record.
  • Marko & Siggia (1995), Stretching DNA. Primary wormlike-chain treatment of DNA stretching; identifies the role of bending stiffness and thermal fluctuations.

43. A sequence acquires a three-dimensional structure

Sources and what they establish
  • Alberts et al. (2002), The Shape and Structure of Proteins. Textbook explanation of sequence, stabilizing interactions, folding, and molecular chaperones.
  • Cooper (2000), Protein Folding and Processing. Textbook distinction between spontaneous refolding in vitro and chaperone-assisted folding in cells.
  • Weinreb et al. (1996), NACP, a protein implicated in Alzheimer’s disease and learning, is natively unfolded. Primary conformational measurements showing a protein can occupy a disordered ensemble under the tested solution conditions; cited only for this structural result.

44. Chemistry that accelerates its own production

Sources and what they establish
  • Lincoln & Joyce (2009), Self-sustained Replication of an RNA Enzyme. Primary experiment: two RNA enzymes cross-catalyze ligation from supplied fragments; sustained amplification requires substrate replenishment.
  • Hordijk & Steel (2012), Autocatalytic Sets Extended: Dynamics, Inhibition, and a Generalization. Primary theoretical analysis; distinguishes structural autocatalytic-set criteria from dynamical and inhibitory constraints.

45. How one molecular handedness can dominate

Sources and what they establish
  • Soai et al. (1995), Asymmetric autocatalysis and amplification of enantiomeric excess of a chiral molecule. Primary chemical experiment demonstrating amplification of initial enantiomeric excess; not a reconstruction of prebiotic chemistry.
  • Frank (1953), On spontaneous asymmetric synthesis. Primary theoretical reaction scheme for symmetry breaking through autocatalysis and mutual inhibition.

46. A cell remembers which genes were active

Sources and what they establish
  • Gardner, Cantor & Collins (2000), Construction of a genetic toggle switch in Escherichia coli. Primary engineered two-repressor switch with a mathematical bistability model; general cell-fate claims require additional biological evidence.

47. A day emerges from molecular reaction cycles

Sources and what they establish
  • Nakajima et al. (2005), Reconstitution of circadian oscillation of cyanobacterial KaiC phosphorylation in vitro. Primary reconstitution experiment using KaiA, KaiB, KaiC and ATP; tests temperature compensation and mutant periods.
  • Nishiwaki et al. (2007), A sequential program of dual phosphorylation of KaiC as a basis for circadian rhythm in cyanobacteria. Primary experiment identifying an ordered phosphorylation cycle; does not establish one universal clock mechanism across organisms.

48. Chemical position becomes organized form

Sources and what they establish
  • Gregor et al. (2007), Probing the limits to positional information. Primary measurements of Bicoid concentration, fluctuations, target response, and embryo-to-embryo reproducibility; the exponential profile in this entry is an explicit illustrative model.

49. A changing cell maintains a stable output

Sources and what they establish
  • Barkai & Leibler (1997), Robustness in simple biochemical networks. Primary theoretical chemotaxis model establishing robust adaptation through network structure.
  • Yi et al. (2000), Robust perfect adaptation in bacterial chemotaxis through integral feedback control. Primary mathematical interpretation of the adaptation mechanism; exact tracking assumes stable operation and the model constraints.

50. When a cluster becomes a reproducing individual

Sources and what they establish
  • Ratcliff et al. (2012), Experimental evolution of multicellularity. Primary settling-selection experiment in snowflake yeast; supports postdivision attachment, cluster reproduction, and evolved group traits, not a complete account of animal origins.

Ecology and evolution: collectives with histories

51. Population change without a population planner

Sources and what they establish
  • Lenski & Travisano (1994), Dynamics of adaptation and diversification: a 10,000-generation experiment with bacterial populations. Primary longitudinal experiment measuring adaptation relative to an ancestor and divergence among replicate E. coli populations; not evidence of universal evolutionary progress.

52. A new function depends on the route taken

Sources and what they establish
  • Blount, Borland & Lenski (2008), Historical contingency and the evolution of a key innovation in an experimental population of Escherichia coli. Primary replay experiment establishing dependence of citrate-use innovation on prior evolutionary history.
  • Quandt et al. (2015), Fine-tuning citrate synthase flux potentiates and refines metabolic innovation in the Lenski evolution experiment. Primary molecular analysis of changes before and after citrate-use innovation; supports a multistep, context-dependent mechanism.

53. A shared benefit survives competition

Sources and what they establish
  • Gore, Youk & van Oudenaarden (2009), Snowdrift game dynamics and facultative cheating in yeast. Primary experiment showing frequency-dependent coexistence of invertase producers and nonproducers; cooperation outcomes depend on nonlinear benefits and growth conditions.
  • Ohtsuki et al. (2006), A simple rule for the evolution of cooperation on graphs and social networks. Primary theoretical work on structured populations; graph-based cooperation criteria are conditional on the update rule and model assumptions.

54. Similar individuals acquire different jobs

Sources and what they establish
  • Ulrich et al. (2018), Fitness benefits and emergent division of labour at the onset of group living. Primary tracking experiment and model in clonal ant groups; separates increased group size from specialization as contributors to colony performance.

55. A moving group finds a common direction

Sources and what they establish
  • Ballerini et al. (2008), Interaction ruling animal collective behavior depends on topological rather than metric distance: Evidence from a field study. Primary field study, author preprint; infers a finite neighbor-rank interaction range from starling flock structure and compares cohesion in models.
  • Vicsek et al. (1995), Novel type of phase transition in a system of self-driven particles. Primary minimal alignment model; establishes model-level collective order, not a complete behavioral model of birds.

56. Persistence depends on who affects whom

Sources and what they establish
  • May (1972), Will a Large Complex System be Stable?. Primary random-matrix stability analysis; applies to local stability of specified random ensembles.
  • Allesina & Tang (2012), Stability criteria for complex ecosystems. Primary analytical extension showing effects of correlated interaction signs and strengths; does not identify diversity with stability in empirical ecosystems.

57. A system can look steady while losing its ability to recover

Sources and what they establish
  • Carpenter et al. (2011), Early warnings of regime shifts: a whole-ecosystem experiment. Primary lake manipulation with an adjacent reference ecosystem; observes warning statistics during food-web reorganization.
  • Veraart et al. (2012), Recovery rates reflect distance to a tipping point in a living system. Primary cyanobacterial microcosm experiment testing recovery rates and autocorrelation; timing of a transition remains stochastic.

58. Today’s community changes tomorrow’s possibilities

Sources and what they establish
  • Connell & Slatyer (1977), Mechanisms of Succession in Natural Communities and Their Role in Community Stability and Organization. Primary conceptual model paper defining facilitation, tolerance, and inhibition; sequence alone does not establish the operating mechanism.

59. Individual transmission produces a population threshold

Sources and what they establish
  • Kermack & McKendrick (1927), A contribution to the mathematical theory of epidemics. Primary epidemic theory; the constant-rate SIR equations here are a special case, not the entirety of the original infection-age formulation.
  • Kermack & McKendrick (1991 reprint), Contributions to the mathematical theory of epidemics—I. 1927. Accessible abstract of the primary historical paper emphasizes homogeneous-population assumptions and epidemic thresholds.

60. A community creates metabolic opportunities

Sources and what they establish
  • Rakoff-Nahoum, Foster & Comstock (2016), The evolution of cooperation within the gut microbiota. Primary enzyme-deletion, culture, and defined-mouse-community experiments supporting specific reciprocal benefits; not a general clinical microbiome claim.

Brains: collective dynamics and the limits of explanation

61. Ion channels create a traveling electrical event

Sources and what they establish
  • Hodgkin & Huxley (1952), A quantitative description of membrane current and its application to conduction and excitation in nerve. Primary squid-axon conductance model; historical full text is scanned.
  • Gerstner et al. (2014), Neuronal Dynamics, section 2.2: Hodgkin–Huxley Model. Author-hosted textbook explanation of capacitive membrane dynamics, sodium and potassium gating, and refractory behavior.

62. A network develops a rhythm its cells need not share

Sources and what they establish
  • Wilson & Cowan (1972), Excitatory and inhibitory interactions in localized populations of model neurons. Primary population-model paper demonstrating limit cycles and multiple stable states; the entry labels its equations as a simplified rate-model form.
  • Gerstner et al. (2014), Neuronal Dynamics, section 20.2: Oscillations: good or bad?. Author-hosted textbook distinguishes network oscillations, single-cell firing, and stability of asynchronous activity.

63. An ensemble represents what one neuron cannot specify

Sources and what they establish
  • Georgopoulos, Kettner & Schwartz (1988), Primate motor cortex and free arm movements to visual targets in three-dimensional space. II. Coding of the direction of movement by a neuronal population. Primary recording study using population vectors from directionally tuned cells; supports decodability, not exclusive coding or proof of the downstream decoding mechanism.
  • Moreno-Bote et al. (2014), Information-limiting correlations. Primary analytical and numerical work showing that particular noise correlations aligned with signal changes limit population information; correlations are not uniformly harmful.

64. A partial cue can recover a whole pattern

Sources and what they establish
  • Hopfield (1982), Neural networks and physical systems with emergent collective computational abilities. Primary content-addressable memory model; convergence claims depend on symmetric connections and asynchronous updating.
  • Gerstner et al. (2014), Neuronal Dynamics, section 17.2: Hopfield Model. Author-hosted textbook derivation of energy descent, retrieval, capacity limits, and additional fixed points.

65. Noisy measurements combine into a percept

Sources and what they establish
  • Ernst & Banks (2002), Humans integrate visual and haptic information in a statistically optimal fashion. Primary controlled experiment on visual-touch height judgments; supports near-optimal integration for that task, not a universal Bayesian implementation claim.

66. A noisy stream becomes a discrete choice

Sources and what they establish
  • Shadlen & Newsome (2001), Neural Basis of a Perceptual Decision in the Parietal Cortex (Area LIP) of the Rhesus Monkey. Primary recording study linking task-related neuronal activity to motion decisions; a correlation does not establish necessity.
  • Katz et al. (2016), Dissociated functional significance of decision-related activity in the primate dorsal stream. Primary reversible-inactivation experiment demonstrating a dissociation between decision correlation and causal impact in the tested tasks.

67. Local changes reshape future network responses

Sources and what they establish
  • Bi & Poo (1998), Synaptic modifications in cultured hippocampal neurons: dependence on spike timing, synaptic strength, and postsynaptic cell type. Primary paired-spike experiment; supports timing-dependent changes under specified culture, cell-type, and initial-strength conditions. The exponential window is a schematic model.

68. Neural activity carries relations between places

Sources and what they establish
  • Hafting et al. (2005), Microstructure of a spatial map in the entorhinal cortex. Primary grid-cell recording study; establishes spatial lattice-like firing and landmark relationships, while path-integration implementation remains an interpretation.
  • O’Keefe & Dostrovsky (1971), The hippocampus as a spatial map. Preliminary evidence from unit activity in the freely-moving rat. Primary early place-cell recording study; supports spatially selective activity, not the full mechanism of navigation.

69. Do neural cascades identify a critical state?

Sources and what they establish
  • Beggs & Plenz (2003), Neuronal avalanches in neocortical circuits. Primary cortical-tissue recording study reporting avalanche statistics and a criticality interpretation.
  • Touboul & Destexhe (2017), Power-law statistics and universal scaling in the absence of criticality. Primary mathematical and numerical counterexample: noncritical dynamics can display power laws and scaling; such statistics alone are insufficient evidence.

70. Explaining experience requires more than naming emergence

Sources and what they establish
  • Casali et al. (2013), A theoretically based index of consciousness independent of sensory processing and behavior. Primary perturbational-complexity experiment across specified human states; its empirical discrimination does not establish a universal measure or prove a theory of experience.
  • Cogitate Consortium et al. (2025), Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness. Primary preregistered multimodal comparison; reports both supported and challenged predictions, with limits on theory-level inference.

Shared expectations and collective outcomes

71. How a word becomes shared

Sources and what they establish
  • Centola and Baronchelli (2015), The spontaneous emergence of conventions: An experimental study of cultural evolution. Controlled human naming-game experiment showing network-dependent global conventions; supports a bounded coordination mechanism, not the origin of natural language as a whole.

72. Why expectations can sustain a rule

Sources and what they establish
  • Bicchieri and Xiao (2009), Do the right thing: But only if others do so. Primary allocation experiment separating empirical and normative expectations; conclusions are restricted to its design and participants.

73. A price coordinates dispersed decisions

Sources and what they establish
  • Smith (1962), An Experimental Study of Competitive Market Behavior. Primary laboratory auction study of competitive-price predictions; it does not establish efficiency of all market institutions or real-world markets.

74. An object accepted because others accept it

Sources and what they establish
  • Kiyotaki and Wright (1993), A Search-Theoretic Approach to Monetary Economics. Primary search model of monetary exchange and the double-coincidence problem; an equilibrium mechanism, not a universal historical account of money.

75. Local choices can produce a separated map

Sources and what they establish
  • Schelling (1971), Dynamic models of segregation. Primary stylized model of interactive relocation; demonstrates possible collective outcomes without identifying the causes of any particular city’s segregation.

76. A jam can travel while cars move forward

Sources and what they establish
  • Sugiyama et al. (2008), Traffic jams without bottlenecks—experimental evidence for the physical mechanism of the formation of a jam. Primary circular-track experiment demonstrating spontaneous jam formation without a fixed bottleneck; not a complete model of multilane road traffic.

77. When several imperfect judgments improve an answer

Sources and what they establish
  • Woolley et al. (2010), Evidence for a collective intelligence factor in the performance of human groups. Primary small-group experiments reporting a common statistical performance factor; correlations do not by themselves establish its causal origin.
  • Bates and Gupta (2017), Smart groups of smart people: Evidence for IQ as the origin of collective intelligence in the performance of human groups. Primary group experiments finding stronger explanatory roles for individual cognitive ability and challenging some predictors in the earlier account.

78. A shared resource need not be unmanaged

Sources and what they establish
  • Ostrom (2010), Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems. Authoritative synthesis by the primary researcher of field, theoretical, and experimental work on varied governance arrangements; no universal institutional recipe is inferred.

79. Rules that persist beyond their participants

Sources and what they establish
  • North (1991), Institutions. Conceptual and historical account of formal and informal constraints and institutional persistence; not an experimentally identified universal causal law.
  • Ostrom (2010), Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems. Comparative governance evidence supports the importance of monitoring, rules, and enforcement arrangements; its applications remain context dependent.

80. When interaction leaves groups farther apart

Sources and what they establish
  • Hegselmann and Krause (2002), Opinion dynamics and bounded confidence: models, analysis and simulation. Primary mathematical and simulation study of bounded-confidence opinion models; the update assumptions are stylized rather than an identified universal mechanism of political behavior.
  • Bail et al. (2018), Exposure to opposing views on social media can increase political polarization. Primary Twitter field experiment with opposing-elite messages; asymmetric partisan results and selected participants limit generalization.
  • Nyhan et al. (2023), Like-minded sources on Facebook are prevalent but not polarizing. Primary Facebook intervention during the 2020 US election; reduced like-minded exposure did not measurably alter its preregistered attitudes, without ruling out every platform effect.

Connectivity, reliable information, and learned structure

81. A system becomes connected

Sources and what they establish
  • Broadbent and Hammersley (1957), Percolation processes. I. Crystals and mazes. Primary formulation of percolation through a random medium; the tree calculation in the text is an elementary illustrative derivation.
  • Bollobás and Riordan (2006), Percolation. Authoritative mathematical text; its preface distinguishes rigorous lattice results from heuristic and conjectural claims.

82. How unequal connectivity can grow

Sources and what they establish
  • Barabási and Albert (1999), Emergence of scaling in random networks. Primary growing-network model combining growth and linear preferential attachment; its power law is a model result.
  • Broido and Clauset (2019), Scale-free networks are rare. Primary statistical analysis of empirical network datasets challenging the prevalence of strongly scale-free structure under defined tests.

83. Why a small failure can become a large one

Sources and what they establish
  • Watts (2002), A simple model of global cascades on random networks. Primary binary-threshold network model with cascade windows; infrastructure and social applications require separately justified transmission rules.

84. Agreement despite incomplete local knowledge

Sources and what they establish
  • Fischer, Lynch, and Paterson (1985), Impossibility of distributed consensus with one faulty process. Primary impossibility theorem for deterministic consensus under full asynchrony and a possible crash; does not rule out all practical consensus protocols.
  • Lamport, Shostak, and Pease (1982), The Byzantine Generals Problem. Primary analysis of arbitrary faulty behavior under explicit communication assumptions; thresholds depend on the model and message authentication.

85. Reliable information from unreliable symbols

Sources and what they establish
  • Hamming (1950), Error Detecting and Error Correcting Codes. Primary construction of error-detecting and correcting binary codes; the repetition-code probability is an elementary independent-noise example.

86. Computation carried by moving patterns

Sources and what they establish
  • Cook (2004), Universality in Elementary Cellular Automata. Primary proof of Rule 110 universality; the construction uses prepared configurations and does not establish spontaneous useful computation from random starts.
  • Cook (2009), A Concrete View of Rule 110 Computation. Author’s explicit account of encoding computation with repeating left and right backgrounds and a central pattern.

87. Local rules that recover a global arrangement

Sources and what they establish
  • Dijkstra (1974), Self-stabilizing systems in spite of distributed control. Primary ring constructions and definition of self-stabilization, using a central scheduler that chooses individual enabled moves; changed scheduling assumptions require new analysis.

88. Useful features that were not specified one by one

Sources and what they establish
  • Rumelhart, Hinton, and Williams (1986), Learning representations by back-propagating errors. Primary demonstration of error-driven training producing useful hidden representations; does not establish biological backpropagation or semantic interpretations of every unit.

89. An abrupt score can hide gradual learning

Sources and what they establish
  • Power et al. (2022), Grokking: Generalization Beyond Overfitting on Small Algorithmic Datasets. Primary computational experiments documenting delayed generalization in controlled algorithmic tasks.
  • Nanda et al. (2023), Progress measures for grokking via mechanistic interpretability. Primary mechanistic case study of modular-addition networks, identifying gradual internal progress and testing components by ablation; not a universal account of all learning.

90. When an ability seems to appear from nowhere

Sources and what they establish
  • Wei et al. (2022), Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models. Primary synthesis of reported scaling-dependent task abilities and their proposed operational definition of emergence.
  • Schaeffer, Miranda, and Koyejo (2023), Are Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models a Mirage?. Primary metric-based alternative with mathematical examples and empirical analyses; challenges abruptness in examined cases without proving all internal learning changes are smooth.

Landscapes, planets, and the cosmos

91. Dunes: a hill made of through-traffic

Sources and what they establish
  • Claudin and Andreotti (2006), A scaling law for aeolian dunes on Mars, Venus, Earth, and for subaqueous ripples. Primary analysis and cross-environment wavelength comparison; the drag-length scaling has stated transport assumptions.
  • Andreotti and Claudin (2013), Aeolian and subaqueous bedforms in shear flows. Review of flow–bed instability, transport adjustment, and nonlinear development.

92. River networks: flow rewrites its own map

Sources and what they establish
  • Howard (1994), A detachment-limited model of drainage basin evolution. Primary dynamical model of coupled channel and hillslope development; not a claim of universal exponents.
  • Rodríguez-Iturbe et al. (1992), Energy dissipation, runoff production, and the three-dimensional structure of river basins. Primary study connecting basin geometry, runoff, and energy dissipation; complements rather than replaces erosion dynamics.

93. Cloud organization: storms make their own neighborhood

Sources and what they establish
  • Wing et al. (2017), Convective self-aggregation in numerical simulations: a review. Review of moisture, radiation, surface-flux, and circulation feedbacks in idealized models.
  • Holloway (2017), Convective aggregation in realistic convective-scale simulations. Primary mechanism-denial comparisons in realistic tropical cases; evidence for connections with idealized aggregation, with limits.

94. Weather: a reproducible climate need not repeat its days

Sources and what they establish
  • Lorenz (1963), Deterministic nonperiodic flow. Primary low-dimensional convection model and implications for predictability; not a full atmospheric calculation.
  • IPCC (2021), AR6 Working Group I, Chapter 4: Future global climate. Assessment of climate projections, internal variability, and forced response; distinguishes climate statistics from individual weather events.

95. Climate feedbacks: the thermostat is a coupled system

Sources and what they establish
  • IPCC (2021), AR6 Working Group I, Chapter 7: The Earth’s energy budget, climate feedbacks, and climate sensitivity. Assessment of forcing, feedback decomposition, and equilibrium versus transient responses; the displayed one-box solution is an explanatory derivation.
  • Geoffroy et al. (2013), Transient climate response in a two-layer energy-balance model, Part I. Primary calibration of a two-layer model and its fast and slow response modes.

96. Ice–albedo feedback: a system can remember

Sources and what they establish
  • IPCC (2021), AR6 Working Group I, Chapter 7, §7.4.2.3: Surface-albedo feedback. Assessment of the positive snow and ice reflectivity feedback; distinguishes surface albedo from planetary albedo.
  • Budyko (1969), The effect of solar radiation variations on the climate of the Earth. Classic energy-balance model connecting ice cover and absorbed sunlight; model transitions require its assumptions.

97. Plate tectonics: solid rock learns to flow

Sources and what they establish
  • USGS, What is a tectonic plate?. Authoritative explanation of lithosphere and plates.
  • USGS, Are the tectonic plates floating on magma?. Clarifies the solid but deformable material beneath plates.
  • USGS, Understanding plate motions. Observational and geological description of divergent, convergent, and transform boundaries.
  • Bercovici and Ricard (2014), Plate tectonics, damage and inheritance. Primary model coupling damage and grain evolution to strain localization; one proposed mechanism, not a unique demonstrated history of Earth.

98. Planetary rings: a disk develops a collective voice

Sources and what they establish
  • Salo (1992), Gravitational wakes in Saturn’s rings. Primary simulations with mutual gravity and dissipative collisions.
  • Hedman et al. (2007), Self-gravity wake structures in Saturn’s A ring revealed by Cassini VIMS. Primary occultation evidence constraining wake structure and viewing-angle effects.
  • NASA Cassini, Staggering structure. Mission explanation of the observed Janus-driven spiral density wave.

99. Gravitational structure: attraction builds a cosmic web

Sources and what they establish
  • Springel et al. (2005), Simulating the joint evolution of quasars, galaxies and their large-scale distribution. Primary large-volume calculation of hierarchical dark-matter structure and modeled luminous populations.
  • Bertschinger (1995), Cosmological dynamics. Lecture notes on perturbation growth, gravitational dynamics, and their cosmological assumptions.

100. Emergent spacetime: a frontier, not a finished example

Sources and what they establish
  • Ryu and Takayanagi (2006), Holographic derivation of entanglement entropy from AdS/CFT. Primary area–entropy proposal in holographic theories, with a specified classical gravitational setting.
  • Van Raamsdonk (2010), Building up spacetime with quantum entanglement. Argument linking entanglement and semiclassical connected geometry; a theoretical frontier, not direct evidence about our universe.
