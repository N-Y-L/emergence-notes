Sources and scope

References are grouped by chapter and entry. Each annotation identifies the result or explanation supported by the source. Original papers appear alongside reviews and lecture notes.

The collection compares mechanisms across fields. Its broad motivation is Anderson’s “More Is Different” (1972).

References

References

30. Topological bands and protected boundaries

References

Living matter: structures that maintain and reproduce themselves

41. Molecular self-assembly: Membranes from molecular interactions

References
  • Israelachvili, Mitchell & Ninham (1976), Theory of self-assembly of hydrocarbon amphiphiles into micelles and bilayers. Connects aggregate free energies and molecular packing constraints to the formation of micelles and bilayers.
  • Alberts et al. (2002), The Chemical Components of a Cell. Explains amphiphilic cell components and the noncovalent interactions underlying molecular assembly.

42. Polymer elasticity: A molecular chain becomes an entropic spring

References
  • Bustamante et al. (1994), Entropic elasticity of lambda-phage DNA. Discusses the entropic elasticity of stretched DNA; the ideal-chain derivation in this entry is an illustrative statistical-mechanical calculation.
  • Marko & Siggia (1995), Stretching DNA. Develops the wormlike-chain description of DNA stretching, including bending stiffness and thermal fluctuations.

43. Protein folding: A sequence forms a reproducible structure

References
  • Alberts et al. (2002), The Shape and Structure of Proteins. Explains protein sequence, stabilizing interactions, structural ensembles, and the role of molecular chaperones.
  • Cooper (2000), Protein Folding and Processing. Describes spontaneous refolding in solution and folding assistance within cells.
  • Weinreb et al. (1996), NACP, a protein implicated in Alzheimer’s disease and learning, is natively unfolded. Reports conformational measurements showing that the studied protein occupies a disordered ensemble under the tested solution conditions.

44. Autocatalysis: Chemistry that accelerates its own production

References
  • Lincoln & Joyce (2009), Self-sustained Replication of an RNA Enzyme. Demonstrates two RNA catalysts assembling copies of one another from supplied fragments, with continued amplification through substrate replenishment.
  • Hordijk & Steel (2012), Autocatalytic Sets Extended: Dynamics, Inhibition, and a Generalization. Analyzes molecular-flow dynamics and inhibition in networks satisfying autocatalytic-set criteria.

45. Homochirality: Amplification of molecular handedness

References
  • Soai et al. (1995), Asymmetric autocatalysis and amplification of enantiomeric excess of a chiral molecule. Demonstrates amplification of an initial enantiomeric excess in an asymmetric autocatalytic reaction.
  • Frank (1953), On spontaneous asymmetric synthesis. Introduces a reaction scheme in which autocatalysis and mutual inhibition destabilize a symmetric mixture.

46. Gene switches: Chemical feedback stores a cell state

References
  • Gardner, Cantor & Collins (2000), Construction of a genetic toggle switch in Escherichia coli. Demonstrates an engineered two-repressor switch in E. coli and models the conditions supporting bistability.

47. Biological clocks: A daily rhythm from molecular reactions

References
  • Nakajima et al. (2005), Reconstitution of circadian oscillation of cyanobacterial KaiC phosphorylation in vitro. Reconstitutes KaiC phosphorylation rhythms with KaiA, KaiB, KaiC, and ATP, and examines temperature dependence and mutant periods.
  • Nishiwaki et al. (2007), A sequential program of dual phosphorylation of KaiC as a basis for circadian rhythm in cyanobacteria. Identifies the ordered cycle of changes at two KaiC phosphorylation sites and their relation to clock regulation.

48. Morphogenesis: From chemical gradients to biological form

References
  • Gregor et al. (2007), Probing the limits to positional information. Measures Bicoid concentrations, fluctuations, target-gene responses, and reproducibility among embryos; the exponential profile here is an illustrative model.

49. Cellular homeostasis: Feedback maintains a stable cellular output

References
  • Barkai & Leibler (1997), Robustness in simple biochemical networks. Shows how a chemotaxis network can achieve adaptation that is robust to changes in model parameters.
  • Yi et al. (2000), Robust perfect adaptation in bacterial chemotaxis through integral feedback control. Connects chemotactic adaptation to integral feedback and identifies the conditions needed for exact steady-state adaptation.

50. Multicellularity: A cluster acquires a reproducing life cycle

References
  • Ratcliff et al. (2012), Experimental evolution of multicellularity. Documents settling selection, persistent attachment after cell division, cluster reproduction, and evolved group traits in snowflake yeast.

Ecology and evolution: collectives with histories

51. Natural selection: Heritable differences change a population

References
  • Lenski & Travisano (1994), Dynamics of adaptation and diversification: a 10,000-generation experiment with bacterial populations. Measures adaptation relative to an ancestor and divergence among replicate E. coli populations over ten thousand generations.

52. Evolutionary innovation: New capabilities depend on evolutionary history

References
  • Blount, Borland & Lenski (2008), Historical contingency and the evolution of a key innovation in an experimental population of Escherichia coli. Uses replay experiments from preserved bacterial populations to test how prior evolution changes access to citrate use.
  • Quandt et al. (2015), Fine-tuning citrate synthase flux potentiates and refines metabolic innovation in the Lenski evolution experiment. Identifies changes in citrate synthase activity that contributed to the preparation for, and refinement of, citrate-use metabolism.

53. Cooperation: A shared resource can sustain its producers

References
  • Gore, Youk & van Oudenaarden (2009), Snowdrift game dynamics and facultative cheating in yeast. Shows frequency-dependent coexistence of invertase producers and nonproducers, linked to nonlinear growth benefits and partial retention of released food.
  • Ohtsuki et al. (2006), A simple rule for the evolution of cooperation on graphs and social networks. Derives cooperation criteria for specified interaction graphs, selection strengths, and population update rules.

54. Division of labor: Task specialization among similar individuals

References
  • Ulrich et al. (2018), Fitness benefits and emergent division of labour at the onset of group living. Tracks specialization in clonal ant groups and uses a model to separate effects of group size and division of labor on colony stability.

55. Collective motion: Local interactions coordinate a moving group

References
  • Ballerini et al. (2008), Interaction ruling animal collective behavior depends on topological rather than metric distance: Evidence from a field study. Infers a neighbor-rank interaction range from three-dimensional starling flock structure and compares cohesion under alternative model rules.
  • Vicsek et al. (1995), Novel type of phase transition in a system of self-driven particles. Demonstrates collective directional order in a minimal model of moving particles with local alignment and angular noise.

56. Food webs: Interaction structure controls community stability

References
  • May (1972), Will a Large Complex System be Stable?. Derives a local stability criterion for large random community matrices with stabilizing diagonal terms.
  • Allesina & Tang (2012), Stability criteria for complex ecosystems. Shows how correlations in the signs and strengths of reciprocal interactions change random-matrix stability criteria.

57. Ecosystem resilience: Recovery rates and the size of a disturbance

References
  • Carpenter et al. (2011), Early warnings of regime shifts: a whole-ecosystem experiment. Reports warning statistics during a manipulated lake food-web transition, with a reference lake for comparison.
  • Veraart et al. (2012), Recovery rates reflect distance to a tipping point in a living system. Tests recovery rates and temporal correlations in cyanobacterial populations approaching a light-induced collapse threshold.

58. Ecological succession: Community change depends on earlier occupants

References
  • Connell & Slatyer (1977), Mechanisms of Succession in Natural Communities and Their Role in Community Stability and Organization. Formulates facilitation, tolerance, and inhibition as alternative mechanisms of succession and identifies ways to distinguish them.

59. Epidemics: Transmission produces a population threshold

References
  • Kermack & McKendrick (1927), A contribution to the mathematical theory of epidemics. Develops epidemic thresholds in a formulation that includes the time since infection; constant-rate SIR is a special case.
  • Kermack & McKendrick (1991 reprint), Contributions to the mathematical theory of epidemics—I. 1927. Reprints the original epidemic theory, including its homogeneous-population assumptions and threshold behavior.
  • Diekmann, Heesterbeek & Roberts (2010), The construction of next-generation matrices for compartmental epidemic models. Constructs next-generation matrices and distinguishes reproduction number from exponential growth rate in time.

60. Microbiome mutualism: Resource exchange creates community capabilities

References
  • Rakoff-Nahoum, Foster & Comstock (2016), The evolution of cooperation within the gut microbiota. Uses enzyme deletions, cultures, and communities in mice to test cross-feeding and reciprocal competitive benefits between gut bacteria.

Brains: collective dynamics and the limits of explanation

61. Action potentials: Ion channels generate a traveling electrical pulse

References
  • Hodgkin & Huxley (1952), A quantitative description of membrane current and its application to conduction and excitation in nerve. Relates measured squid-axon ionic conductances to membrane excitation and action-potential propagation.
  • Gerstner et al. (2014), Neuronal Dynamics, section 2.2: Hodgkin–Huxley Model. Derives capacitive membrane dynamics and explains sodium and potassium gating, propagation prerequisites, and refractory behavior.

62. Neural oscillations: Excitation and inhibition generate a network rhythm

References
  • Wilson & Cowan (1972), Excitatory and inhibitory interactions in localized populations of model neurons. Demonstrates steady states, multiple stable states, and limit cycles in interacting excitatory and inhibitory populations.
  • Gerstner et al. (2014), Neuronal Dynamics, section 20.2: Oscillations: good or bad?. Explains network oscillations, their relation to single-cell firing, and the stability of asynchronous population activity.

63. Population coding: A variable is represented across many neurons

References
  • Georgopoulos, Kettner & Schwartz (1988), Primate motor cortex and free arm movements to visual targets in three-dimensional space. II. Coding of the direction of movement by a neuronal population. Tests population-vector estimates of three-dimensional reach direction using directionally tuned motor-cortex neurons.
  • Moreno-Bote et al. (2014), Information-limiting correlations. Shows how noise correlations that resemble changes in the encoded variable can limit information in a neural population.

64. Attractor memory: Network dynamics completes a stored pattern

References
  • Hopfield (1982), Neural networks and physical systems with emergent collective computational abilities. Introduces distributed pattern storage and retrieval through collective dynamics, with convergence under the specified symmetric asynchronous update rule.
  • Gerstner et al. (2014), Neuronal Dynamics, section 17.2: Hopfield Model. Derives energy descent and explains retrieval, storage limits, and unintended fixed points in Hopfield networks.

65. Perceptual inference: Combining uncertain sensory estimates

References
  • Ernst & Banks (2002), Humans integrate visual and haptic information in a statistically optimal fashion. Measures visual and touch uncertainty separately and tests the predicted reliability-weighted combination in height judgments.

66. Evidence accumulation and discrete choices

References
  • Shadlen & Newsome (2001), Neural Basis of a Perceptual Decision in the Parietal Cortex (Area LIP) of the Rhesus Monkey. Records parietal neuronal responses associated with motion judgments and examines their relation to evidence accumulation.
  • Katz et al. (2016), Dissociated functional significance of decision-related activity in the primate dorsal stream. Compares recordings and reversible inactivation in sensory motion and parietal areas, finding different causal effects despite strong decision correlations.

67. Synaptic learning: Activity changes the connections that shape future activity

References
  • Bi & Poo (1998), Synaptic modifications in cultured hippocampal neurons: dependence on spike timing, synaptic strength, and postsynaptic cell type. Measures how repeated paired spikes change synapses as a function of timing, initial strength, and receiving cell type; the exponential rule here is schematic.

68. Cognitive maps: Population activity represents spatial position

References
  • Hafting et al. (2005), Microstructure of a spatial map in the entorhinal cortex. Documents triangular spatial firing patterns, shared grid spacing and orientation, differing phases, and relationships to landmarks.
  • O’Keefe & Dostrovsky (1971), The hippocampus as a spatial map. Preliminary evidence from unit activity in the freely-moving rat. Reports location-selective hippocampal activity during movement in rats.

69. Brain criticality: Neural cascades and the criticality hypothesis

References
  • Beggs & Plenz (2003), Neuronal avalanches in neocortical circuits. Reports avalanche-size statistics and branching estimates in rat neocortical preparations.
  • Touboul & Destexhe (2017), Power-law statistics and universal scaling in the absence of criticality. Constructs noncritical network and stochastic models with power-law avalanche statistics and scaling signatures.

70. Consciousness and neural organization

References
  • Casali et al. (2013), A theoretically based index of consciousness independent of sensory processing and behavior. Tests a complexity measure of cortical responses to magnetic stimulation across specified human conscious states and clinical conditions.
  • Cogitate Consortium et al. (2025), Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness. Compares preregistered predictions from two consciousness theories using several neural measurement methods, supporting some predictions and challenging others.

Expectations, rules, and collective outcomes

72. Social norms and expectations

References

73. Market prices: Price formation in an auction

References

74. Money and shared acceptance

References
  • Kiyotaki and Wright (1993), A Search-Theoretic Approach to Monetary Economics. Search model in which shared acceptance supports monetary exchange despite the difficulty of matching wants.

75. Residential segregation from local relocation rules

References
  • Schelling (1971), Dynamic models of segregation. Relocation models demonstrating that locally acceptable neighborhood mixtures can generate substantial aggregate segregation.

76. Traffic jams as traveling density patterns

References
  • Sugiyama et al. (2008), Traffic jams without bottlenecks—experimental evidence for the physical mechanism of the formation of a jam. Circular-track experiment in which a traffic jam forms without a fixed bottleneck.

77. Collective intelligence: Collective judgment and correlated errors

References
  • Woolley et al. (2010), Evidence for a collective intelligence factor in the performance of human groups. Small-group experiments reporting a common statistical factor across task performance.

78. Commons governance: Governing a shared resource

References
  • Ostrom (2010), Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems. Synthesis of field studies, models, and experiments on how users govern shared resources through varied institutional arrangements.

79. Institutions and their persistence

References
  • North (1991), Institutions. Account of formal and informal rules, enforcement, and their persistence over historical time.
  • Ostrom (2010), Beyond Markets and States: Polycentric Governance of Complex Economic Systems. Comparative evidence concerning access rules, monitoring, enforcement, and institutional change in resource governance.

80. Polarization and the effects of contact

References
  • Hegselmann and Krause (2002), Opinion dynamics and bounded confidence: models, analysis and simulation. Analysis of opinion clusters generated by bounded-confidence averaging rules.
  • Bail et al. (2018), Exposure to opposing views on social media can increase political polarization. Twitter field experiment reporting an asymmetric response to exposure to opposing political messages.
  • Nyhan et al. (2023), Like-minded sources on Facebook are prevalent but not polarizing. Facebook field experiment finding no measurable change in specified political attitudes after reducing like-minded-source exposure during the 2020 US election.

Networks, computation, and learning

81. Percolation and long-range connectivity

References
  • Broadbent and Hammersley (1957), Percolation processes. I. Crystals and mazes. Formulation of random connectivity through a porous medium; the regular-tree threshold in the text follows from an elementary branching calculation.
  • Bollobás and Riordan (2006), Percolation. Mathematical treatment of percolation thresholds and connectivity, distinguishing proved results from conjectures.

82. Network hubs

References
  • Barabási and Albert (1999), Emergence of scaling in random networks. Growing-network model yielding a power-law degree tail through linear preferential attachment.
  • Broido and Clauset (2019), Scale-free networks are rare. Statistical tests of empirical degree distributions, including comparisons of power-law and alternative fits.

83. Cascading failures

References
  • Watts (2002), A simple model of global cascades on random networks. Binary threshold model showing parameter regimes where a small seed can trigger a macroscopic cascade.

84. Distributed consensus

References
  • Fischer, Lynch, and Paterson (1985), Impossibility of distributed consensus with one faulty process. Impossibility of guaranteed deterministic consensus termination under full asynchrony with even one possible crash.
  • Lamport, Shostak, and Pease (1982), The Byzantine Generals Problem. Agreement with arbitrary faulty behavior under specified communication and authentication assumptions.

85. Error correction through redundancy

References
  • Hamming (1950), Error Detecting and Error Correcting Codes. Binary codes that use parity constraints to detect and correct errors; the repetition-code probability is derived separately in the text.

86. Cellular automata and computation

References
  • Cook (2004), Universality in Elementary Cellular Automata. Proof of computational universality using prepared Rule 110 configurations.
  • Cook (2009), A Concrete View of Rule 110 Computation. Explicit account of the encoding, moving patterns, and repeating backgrounds used to implement computation.

87. Self-stabilization in distributed algorithms

References
  • Dijkstra (1974), Self-stabilizing systems in spite of distributed control. Definition and ring constructions for self-stabilization with one enabled move executed at a time.

88. Learned representations

References
  • Rumelhart, Hinton, and Williams (1986), Learning representations by back-propagating errors. Demonstration that backpropagation can train hidden units to develop task-relevant internal representations.

89. Delayed generalization during training

References
  • Power et al. (2022), Grokking: Generalization Beyond Overfitting on Small Algorithmic Datasets. Experiments documenting delayed generalization on small algorithmically generated datasets.
  • Nanda et al. (2023), Progress measures for grokking via mechanistic interpretability. Modular-addition case study identifying Fourier-based computation, gradual internal development, and the effects of removing selected components.

90. Apparent emergent AI abilities: Capability curves and evaluation thresholds

References
  • Wei et al. (2022), Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models. Definition and reported examples of abilities whose measured performance rises sharply with model scale.
  • Schaeffer, Miranda, and Koyejo (2023), Are Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models a Mirage?. Mathematical and empirical analysis showing how evaluation metrics can create apparent abruptness in capability curves.

Landscapes, planets, and spacetime

91. Dunes: formation and migration

References
  • Claudin and Andreotti (2006), A scaling law for aeolian dunes on Mars, Venus, Earth, and for subaqueous ripples. Linear instability analysis and measurements relating initial bedform wavelengths to the drag length across fluid environments.
  • Andreotti and Claudin (2013), Aeolian and subaqueous bedforms in shear flows. Review of bed–flow instability, transport saturation, and later bedform evolution.

92. River networks and landscape evolution

References
  • Howard (1994), A detachment-limited model of drainage basin evolution. Dynamical model coupling channel incision and hillslope evolution, with channel erosion limited by removal of material from the bed.
  • Rodríguez-Iturbe et al. (1992), Energy dissipation, runoff production, and the three-dimensional structure of river basins. Study of relations among drainage geometry, runoff, and energy dissipation.

93. Cloud organization: Convective self-aggregation

References
